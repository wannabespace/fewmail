# Domain

Fewmail is an **email agent**: it reads every incoming email so the person does not have to, and shows them only what needs them. The UI is built around decisions, not around the inbox.

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Mailbox** | A Gmail account whose mail Fewmail reads. One is the account you sign in with; more can be added in Settings and their mail joins the same Home, queue and inbox; Mail can be narrowed to one of them. An added mailbox never signs anyone in to your account — signing in with it opens its own Fewmail. |
| **Home** | `/home` (`G H`): the live home screen. It updates on its own as mail arrives (no brief, no batches: a card appears as soon as the agent has read the email), and it answers three questions top to bottom, so a visit has an end. **What happened**: a header computed in the browser (no model text): the date, "The agent is reading 3 new emails" while a sync is under way (`reading`: unanalysed inbox mail of the last day, counted only while a mailbox's `syncStartedAt` is set, so a failed analysis never reads forever; sync announces `today` before `processIncoming`), a link to the journal ("What the agent did", `H`, see **Handled**), the headline counting the decisions ("Seven things need you.", or "All done. Nothing needs you." once none is left), one serif sentence ("48 emails in the last day. I read every one, and nothing changes in your mail without your yes. Your rules took care of 6.") and the calendar's events left today and tomorrow as chips linking to that day. While the agent's column is hidden an ask bar under it does what `/` does. **What needs me**: one **Decisions** list with a progress bar ("2 of 7", the decisions settled since the page opened against what is left): fresh sign-in codes first (see **Code**), then the queue, with batch picking: `X` picks the cursor card, `⌘A` picks all, then `⏎` approves, `S` skips and `⌫` deletes the emails of the picked cards; a skip or a delete ends in a toast with **Undo** (`actions.restore` puts a skipped card back, a deleted email returns to the inbox). The cursor holds a card, not a position: a card arriving live above it never takes `⏎`. A card that came after the user's previous look at Home is marked new; a look is `today.open` (`openToday`): opening the page, or coming back to its tab after 10 minutes away, never a live refetch or the agent. Cards waiting more than 7 days are counted under the queue with **Skip them** (`⇧S`). A reply card shows what the sender wrote (the snippet) and who the reply goes to around the draft; **Rewrite** (`R`) takes how to rewrite it in words (empty: shorter). With five or more cards of at least two kinds, pills above the list narrow it to one kind (`F` cycles, "All" first; the cursor, picks and `⏎` work on what is shown). After the queue in the same list: the agent's chats waiting for a yes (opening one in the agent's column), **Tidy** as one card, **new senders**, **rule offers** (each untouched starter rule with a Gmail effect, `listRuleOffers`: Turn on or Keep off, either answer touches the rule so it is asked once), then the board heads-ups. **New sender** (`newSenders` / `keepSender` in `services/cleanup.ts`): a mailing list whose first stored email is at most 14 days old, that wrote at least three times and was opened at most once, with no action on any of its emails, not left, not covered by an enabled `from` or `mailingList` simple rule; at most three. Its card asks once: **Always archive** (`A`, a simple `from` rule, the user's standing yes), **Unsubscribe** (`U`, confirmed first like Subscriptions) or **Keep as usual** (`I`, a `dismissed` action on its latest email, so it is never asked again). **Tidy**: quiet inbox mail (importance < 2) that no rule covers and the agent would archive, mark read or unsubscribe from (`analyses.suggestedAction`, `listTidy` / `settleTidy` in `services/mail.ts`): only a thread's newest email, never a thread with a pending card or an email already acted on. One card counts them per kind; `T` does them all in one yes (a confirmation first when that would leave a mailing list; the archives can be undone), `D` lists each with what and why and a **Keep** that leaves it as it is (a `dismissed` action, so it never comes back); what was done lands as a `done` action beside its email. The agent sees the same list in `get_today` (`tidy`) and carries it out with `tidy_mail` after a yes. Mail analyzed before the column existed has no suggestion. **What is worth knowing**, needing no decision: one **Worth knowing** list of the other unread inbox mail of importance ≥ 1 from the catch-up window, mail of the last 7 days naming a due date or meeting time that no card or code shows (dates as written; an ISO due date sorts soonest first and leaves once passed) and sent mail still awaiting an answer. Then **What your newsletters said**: the opening of the Reading summary and its topic titles in serif, no card, with "12 newsletters today" (`N`) opening Reading; hidden when none came. Last one dashed line: what the agent did for the user in the window (`acted`: `done` and `auto_done` actions) and the time saved, with the journal link. Boards are not on Home (the sidebar has them; `get_today` still returns their digest). Every row below the queue opens its email in Home's reader, never in Mail. **Catch-up window** (`catchUp`, `services/home.ts`): Home covers everything since the user's last day on Home (`user_settings.todaySince`, moved to the previous visit on the first look on a new local day; the agent's `get_today` and `today.get` never count as a visit), at least the last 24 h and at most 7 days, so a few days away are caught up in one sitting; the header sentence says "in the last 3 days". The queue is never windowed. Empty sections hide. The agent has its own column on the right, hidden until the user asks (see **Agent chat**); an email opened from a card (`O`) or an answer opens in a reader over the queue (`?thread=`), and the agent's column stays beside it. Internal names stay `today.*` (oRPC) and `get_today` (MCP clients depend on it). |
| **Queue** | Pending **actions** (`actions.status = 'pending'`), ordered by importance. Each is approved (`⏎`), edited, redrafted or skipped; `⇧⏎` approves every pending card of the selected one's kind at once (`approveActions`: one by one, each send with its own undo window). Batch approval is the answer to repetitive replies: nothing is ever sent without a yes. Telegram and WhatsApp end the queue with one button per kind that has two or more cards (`all:<kind>`). |
| **Action** | Something the agent proposes or did: `reply` (with a draft), `pay`, `schedule`, `follow_up`, `read`, `archive`, `mark_read`, `unsubscribe`, `spam`, `security`, `commitment`, `cleanup`. A card's button never promises what Fewmail does not do. A `pay` or `read` card can carry the email's own link (`payload.link`: pay, sign, renew, confirm or read, shown with its host): ⏎ opens it in a new tab and the yes moves to `M`, saying what the user did ("I paid it", `actionDone`). A `schedule` card whose meeting has an exact start (`payload.event`) is approved as Add to calendar, which creates the event in the email's mailbox calendar. ⏎ on a `follow_up` card writes the reminder, turning it into a `reply` card that still needs its own yes to send. Elsewhere the yes on those kinds only marks the email read and closes the card, and says so ("I scheduled it"). Statuses: `pending`, `done`, `dismissed` (skipped), `auto_done` (by an autonomous rule), `failed`. An email with a **code** never gets a card; the thread view shows each settled card (done, auto_done, failed) beside its own email, never as a list above the thread; pending ones live on Home. |
| **Handled** | What the agent did: `done` (after the user's yes), `auto_done` (by a rule the user turned on, or a sender they reported) and `failed`. Always reviewable, never hidden: Home's **journal** (`H`, `/home?page=journal`, "What the agent did") opens on **Now** (what the agent is reading at the moment and the schedules due in the next 24 h, each opening its chat), then lists the last 7 days by day with the email, why, who allowed it ("You said yes" or "Your rule: …") and an undo where one exists (archive, spam), failures first; the agent reads the same with `list_handled`. Without a yes or a rule the agent only reads. |
| **Time saved** | `timeSaved` (`services/mail.ts`): handled mail × 15 s plus replies sent from the agent's drafts × 3 min (`SECONDS_SAVED`), over the last 7 days. A muted line on Home whose tooltip gives both counts, `saved` in the agent's `get_today`, and on Mondays the **weekly report**: its own message in Telegram and WhatsApp at the morning hour (`weekReply`; outside WhatsApp's 24 h window the template's button `week` fetches it), nothing when no time was saved. Never inflated: only those two counts. |
| **Analysis** | Per-message result (Jev decides; on Pro the fast model writes the text, on Free the summary is the snippet): category, importance 0–3, one-line summary, reason, `needsReply`, extracted amount / due date / meeting time, matched rule. When the LLM writes the text of mail that needs the user (importance ≥ 2), a bill or a ticket (`finance`, `travel`; `worthReading`), the email's PDFs and images (≤ 2, ≤ 10 MB, first 3 pages, `services/attachments.ts`, Mistral OCR) are read into its prompt and never stored. |
| **Importance** | 3 urgent (pushed to Telegram and WhatsApp immediately, each switchable in Settings → Notifications), 2 needs you soon, 1 worth knowing, 0 noise. |
| **Rule** | A plain-language instruction ("Newsletters and digests") + an effect. Gmail effects (`archive`, `mark_read`, `unsubscribe`, `trash`) act on their own and land under handled, except on mail that needs the user (importance ≥ 2: a failed build, a deadline): there the effect waits as a card on Home for a yes, and on Free the email stays in the inbox untouched. Simple rules always act; `draft_reply` leaves a draft on Home, `always_show` pins the email to Home. A rule is on or off (`enabled`); adding one that matches an existing rule's words updates that rule instead of adding a twin. Rules list newest first in a fixed order; simple rules apply the first match in that order. New users get the same starter rules; those with a Gmail effect start switched off, since nobody said yes to them yet, and act only once the user turns them on, from Home's rule offers or Rules (a rule the user turned on or kept is their standing yes). On Free, only the Gmail effects. |
| **Language** | One setting for the whole product, chosen at onboarding (defaults to the browser's): the app UI, Telegram bot, summaries, reasons, action labels and starter rules use it. **Replies always use the language of the email being answered.** The public site follows the visitor's browser language, switchable in its footer, and the choice carries into onboarding. Legal documents stay in English (the binding version) with a translated note. |
| **Voice** | How drafts sound: learned from the user's recent sent mail (synced alongside the inbox) plus an optional `voice` note. |
| **Saved draft** | An unfinished email kept as a Gmail draft, never in our database: the editor saves it on its own a second after each change (no Save button; Discard deletes it), the agent's `create_draft`, or Gmail itself. Listed under Mail → Drafts, reopened in the editor; sending it from Fewmail or discarding it deletes it. Not the agent's reply drafts on Home. Free. |
| **Calendar** | `/calendar` (`G L`): the primary Google calendar of every mailbox, read live and never stored, as a day, week, month or year view. Events are added (`N`, or a press on empty time), edited and deleted there; the agent does the same through `list_events` / `create_event` / `update_event` / `delete_event`, each change waiting for the user's yes. Free. |
| **Folders** | A branch under Mail in the sidebar, open while Mail is (and the Folders group in ⌘K): Snoozed, Drafts, Archive, Spam and Trash open those Mail views (the Mail view picker holds only the inbox views), then the user's own Gmail labels (`mail.labels`, `listUserLabels` in `services/labels.ts`: read live from every mailbox, merged by name, never stored), each opening the Mail search for it (`label:name` in Gmail's own form). Free. |
| **Pin** | Keep a thread in sight: a pin is Gmail's star (`STARRED` on the thread, so starring in Gmail pins too and the other way round). Every inbox view opens on the inbox's pinned threads, one line each and only the first three until "Show all", and the rest of the list leaves them out (`listMail` filter `pinned`, `isPinned` on each row; the agent's `list_mail` takes the same filter). `S` pins or unpins the cursor row or the open thread. Archiving takes a thread off the pinned list but keeps the star. Free. |
| **Snooze** | Hide a thread until a time; it comes back to the inbox unread. Free. |
| **Follow-up** | "Remind me if no reply in N days" on a sent email (Free: the thread resurfaces). The agent also sets follow-ups itself on sent mail that expects an answer (all plans); Pro adds a nudge on Home. |
| **Schedule** | Something the user asked the agent to do regularly ("every Friday at 17:00 collect the week's invoices", "every Monday send the team the report"). Each run is a message in its own agent chat with a notification; anything it wants to send waits for approval there. Created by asking the agent; listed and deleted under **Chats** (`/agent`). |
| **Knowledge** | Free-form facts the user gives the agent (prices, availability…), used in drafts and autocomplete. Pro. |
| **Spam shield** | Community spam protection: "Report spam" (`!`) moves the thread to Gmail spam and votes; enough independent votes flag that sender's mail for everyone. Only a sender the user reported themselves goes to spam on its own (that report was their yes); everything else the shield catches (community votes, spam lists, patterns, two or more weak signals) stays in the inbox marked "Looks like spam" with a pending `spam` card on Home, and its yes moves it and votes (`proposeSpam` / `approveAction`). Votes also count against the campaign's patterns — the same text, the sending network, the linked domains — so a spammer gains nothing by switching address. "Not spam" votes the other way. Free. |
| **Maybe spam** | One weak signal (odd domain name, freshly registered domain, weak sender authentication): labelled "Looks like spam" and kept out of Important, never moved. Two or more → a `spam` card waiting for a yes (see **Spam shield**). Free. |
| **Snippets** | Saved texts inserted with `;name` in the editor. Free. |
| **Agent labels** | Optional `Fewmail/<Category>` Gmail labels so the sorting shows in any Gmail client. Free. |
| **Subscriptions** | `/subscriptions` (`G U`): every mailing list that wrote in the last 30 days and the user has not left, busiest first, with how many emails came and how many were opened (a rule's own archive or mark-read is not an open). A row's name (`⏎` / `O`) opens those emails in Mail. Unsubscribe (`U`) asks first (leaving a list has no undo), keeps the row spinning until the answer and leaves the list the way a person would: RFC 8058 one-click, else its unsubscribe page (opened from our server, a one-click form on it sent, success only when the page says so: `lib/unsubscribe-page.ts`), else its `mailto:` (a request sent from the mailbox the list wrote to; part of the unsubscribe, so it needs no other approval); a page that needs a person comes back as `link` and the user finishes there (a toast in the web, the link in the agent's answer) and archives everything that list sent; only an unsubscribe that went out makes later mail a zombie. A sender the network knows ignores unsubscribes (and without a List-Id) comes back as `ignoresUnsubscribe`: the web asks whether to block it (`offerBlock`, a trash rule for the sender), the agent offers `create_rule`; nothing blocks it unasked. A list is its titled List-Id (each GitHub repository is its own row, named by the List-Id's title when the sender sends several), else its sender. Free. |
| **Clear-out** | Moving the whole inbox backlog out at once (`scanClearOut` / `runClearOut` / `undoClearOut` in `services/cleanup.ts`). Groups (`CLEAR_OUT_GROUPS`): ads and social older than 3 days, read notifications older than 30 days, mailing lists with 3+ emails none opened (found in the newest 600 inbox emails' headers; also unsubscribed), everything older than a year (off by default). Read live from Gmail, headers only, nothing stored; up to 50,000 emails per mailbox. Archived under `Fewmail/Cleared`, so Undo brings every one back (unsubscribes stay). The dialog opens from ⌘K only (never on its own: right after onboarding it fought the first sync for Gmail's quota); the agent has `preview_clear_out`, `clear_out` and `undo_clear_out`. Free. |
| **Wrapped** | `/wrapped` (⌘K, and the Time saved line on Home links there): the year or month so far (`Y` / `M`) in counts: mail in (from people, from lists), handled on its own, replies sent from drafts, time saved, lists left, spam stopped, the busiest weekday and hour, plus the three people who wrote most (in the app only). `getWrapped` in `services/wrapped.ts`; the agent's `get_wrapped`. Shared as a link `/w/<token>` whose token carries the counts signed with HMAC (no table, nothing stored, never a name or address) and as a 1080×1350 PNG drawn in the browser (`I`). Link previews: Caddy sends social crawlers on `/w/*` to the API's `/og/w/<token>` (Open Graph tags) whose image `/og/w/<token>/image.png` is drawn on the server (`services/wrapped-image.ts`, resvg-wasm, Inter and Newsreader from Google Fonts; the token carries the sharer's language, scripts those fonts lack fall back to English). Free. |
| **Reading** | Part of Home: `/home?page=reading` (`N` on Home, `G N` and ⌘K anywhere; `/reading` redirects there for the links in digests already sent), no sidebar row. The last 24 h of newsletters (category `newsletter`), wherever they are (the starter rule archives them). With the agent the page is **one summary of all of them** (`getReadingSummary`, `reading.summary`): an intro, up to four topics whose sentences end on the newsletters they came from (a badge that opens that newsletter), where several cover one thing said once with where they disagree, then up to two worth reading in full with why; promotions and newsletters with nothing new are left out and counted. It is written by one fast-model call from each newsletter's own summary (never the bodies) and kept encrypted in `reading_summaries`; it is written again only when the newsletters changed and it is `SUMMARY_EVERY_HOURS` (4) old, so later arrivals are marked "since the summary" until then. ▶ reads it aloud (`/voice/reading`). The newsletters are listed under it (each marked in the summary, worth reading, left out or since the summary); one opens in Home's reader (`?thread=`), J / K walk the list and move the reader, `esc` goes back to Reading, then to Home. Like Home it is Pro: on Free the newsletters stay in Mail. Each newsletter's own 2–3 sentence summary is written once, in one fast-model call, into the email's `analyses.summary`; on Free it is the snippet. The agent's `get_reading`, and the `reading` skill (`/reading` in chat, Telegram and WhatsApp, an MCP prompt) turns it into a digest. **Newsletter digest** (`readingDigest`, on by default, Settings → Agent): at the morning hour (`briefHour`, 08:00 by default, in the user's time zone; the hour and this switch show in Settings → Agent only while Telegram or WhatsApp is connected) the worker sends the same summary to Telegram and WhatsApp as one message (topic titles in bold) (`readingReply`; outside WhatsApp's 24 h window the template's button `reading` fetches it); nothing when none came. |
| **Voice** | ElevenLabs (`lib/elevenlabs.ts`, `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY`, optional `ELEVENLABS_VOICE_ID`), only on demand: ▶ on Home speaks a live summary, how many things need the user and the first five queue cards (`spokenHome`, MP3 from `/voice/home`); a voice message to the bot is answered with a voice note as well as text (Ogg Opus); the agent chat has a talk button (`⌘⇧Space`: record, `/voice/hear` transcribes, the answer to a spoken turn is read aloud through `/voice/speak`). A failed voice never drops the text. Pro. |
| **Zombie sender** | Mail from a list that keeps coming more than 14 days after you unsubscribed from that list; proposed for spam as a `spam` card, never moved without a yes. Free. |
| **Code** | A sign-in or verification code found in an incoming email (`oneTimeCode`, `apps/api/lib/one-time-code.ts`: a code word in the subject or the body's first 3,000 characters, links removed, then 4 to 8 digits or a short letters-and-digits code beside it; promo, zip and order numbers are not codes), kept encrypted in `messages.code` when the email is stored, before any analysis. The Mail row shows it to copy without opening the email (`Y` on the cursor row), with **Copy and archive** and **Copy and delete** in the chevron menu beside it the way iOS offers to delete a used code. Home opens with the codes of the last 15 minutes still in the inbox (`freshCodes`, at most 3, newest first) with the same split button, `Y` copying the newest, so a code never needs a trip to Mail; the agent's `list_mail` returns it and `get_today` lists the fresh ones (`codes`). Free. |
| **Bulk** | Mail sent near-identically to many people (`bulkCount`). Free. |
| **Risk** | Known phishing domain (public list), lookalike sender domain or changed payment details; shown as a warning and a `security` action. Free. |
| **Commitment** | A promise you made in sent mail; due ones come back as actions. Pro. |
| **People** | Per-contact note the agent keeps, refreshed weekly, shown under a thread with the conversation count, which opens the person's latest 20 threads to jump to. Only for a person: someone the user wrote to or whose mail is `person` / `work` (`isPerson`); a service address gets no panel and no note. Pro. |
| **Board** | A page the user describes in their own words ("Trips: flights and hotels with what they cost"); the agent collects matching mail as **entries** (title, group, status, amount, date), starting with the history of its **period** — how far back it collects, picked when it is created and in its settings (`BOARD_PERIODS`: a day, a week, a month, 3 months (default), 6 months, a year; `boards.periodDays`). Entries that happened before the period leave every view of the board (entries, summary, groups, charts, banners, Home's digest) but stay stored, so a longer period brings them back; a longer period also reads the extra history, a shorter one reads nothing, and picks the **charts** when it is created — the user edits or removes them after. Onboarding offers ready-made **presets** (`BOARD_PRESETS`: Money, Paid subscriptions, Trips, Orders, Events, Invoices, Job search, Health, Home, Courses and school — compact three-column cards; the New board dialog offers the same as one-click fills — up to three, Money and Trips picked by default, kept in `user_settings.starter_boards`); the worker creates them after the first sync as ordinary boards the user may change or delete. Each board may raise **banners**: `planBoard` picks `bannerDays` (renewals and bills 3, trips and events 2, null for none), the extractor records an entry's next date as `dueAt`, and `boardBanners` shows entries due within that window on Home (also in `get_today`) and on top of the board. The page opens on a **summary** (this month against last, what repeats per month, what is due in 30 days), the heads-up banners, the charts, the **groups** (who or what: total, how often it repeats — weekly, monthly, quarterly, yearly, read from steady gaps and a steady price — and the next date, stated in an email or **expected** from that pace) and the entries; clicking a bar, a slice or a group filters the entries. The user corrects an entry by asking the agent (`edit_board_entry`; there is no edit form, the board is not a spreadsheet), takes it off the board (**Not on this board**, undoable; the board never collects that email again and the model learns from it), puts an email the search missed on a board (thread → More → Add to board), renames or merges groups, renames the board in place, changes what it collects (reads the history again; entries the user edited, added or took off stay) and the heads-up days — never the Gmail search itself: `planBoard` writes it from the description, so a hand-edited query cannot break a board, and orders or deletes boards (deleting confirms; it has no undo). Boards live in the sidebar (`G 1`…`G 9`, right-click for the same edits) and in ⌘K. Pro. |
| **Cleanup** | Weekly Sunday suggestion to clear accumulated noise. Pro. |
| **Agent chat** | Lives in Home's right column, hidden until the user asks (the ask bar, `/`, `⌘.` or New chat open it, and it stays as the user left it): the chats on top (the ones waiting for a yes first, then the latest, a schedule's chat with its own glyph), with New chat (`⇧⌘O`) and **All chats** (`/agent`, `G C`: what waits, the schedules, every chat, each deleted there); the open chat under them (`?chat=`); the composer at the bottom (`/` focuses it) with the skills as suggestions under it. The column hides with `⌘.` and comes back the same way, or with `/`. A chat waits while its last turn holds an unanswered approval (`chats.waiting`). It answers from the mail through the same tools as MCP, shows each step it takes, and anything that sends, changes Gmail, skips a card, adds a rule, deletes a board or a schedule or hands a thread over waits for the user's yes on a confirmation card in the chat. The same card holds what an MCP client asks for: such a call never runs on the client's own approval, it opens a chat ("Claude asks through MCP") waiting among Home's decisions, with a push and a Telegram/WhatsApp link. Chats are kept (`chats`) and reopened from Home's column, All chats or a `/home?chat=` link (Telegram, WhatsApp, push, ⌘K's Ask; old `/agent?chat=` links redirect there). Pro. |
| **Skill** | A built-in job the agent knows by name (`SKILLS`: `today`, `waiting`, `followups`, `bills`, `week`, `cleanup`, `reading`, `cancel`: the agent finds a paid service's cancel link or drafts a cancellation email it sends only after a yes; it never signs in or pays), run as `/name` plus optional details. The chat's `/` menu and its suggestions on Home, a Telegram command, a WhatsApp text and an MCP prompt all start the same skill. Not user-defined. Pro. |
| **Instructions** | Free-text or voice messages to the Telegram or WhatsApp bot; replying to a bot message targets its thread. Pro. |

UI copy says "the agent" or "Fewmail", never "AI" or "the model". No em dashes (—) in copy, in any language, nor in prompts and tool descriptions the models read: a comma, colon, period or parentheses instead.

## Plans

| Plan | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Free** | Forever. Mail view, archive / unsubscribe / open in Gmail, **simple rules** (`rules.match`: from, subject contains, any mailing list → archive, mark read, unsubscribe, trash — "Block" is one; always act on their own), and every Jev decision: classification, importance, needs-reply, plain-language rules (archive / mark read / unsubscribe / trash, autonomous unless the mail needs the user), risk checks, zombie and bulk detection, auto follow-ups, agent labels. **No LLM**: no Home, agent chat, summaries, drafts, autocomplete, Ask AI, commitments, money, people, Telegram or WhatsApp. **Shown, not hidden**: Free sees the Pro entry points (Home and Agent in the sidebar and ⌘K, New board, ⌘K Ask, Write for me); using one — a click, its key, or opening its URL — opens the upgrade dialog (what it does on Pro + Upgrade to Pro → Settings → Plan) instead. **MCP** works on Free with the read tools that need no LLM (everything `read` in `AGENT_TOOLS` but `get_person`; no skills as prompts), so a listing in Claude or ChatGPT is a way in; writing and consent tools need the agent. |
| **Trial** | 14 days of Pro from onboarding (`user_settings.trial_ends_at`), no card, with Lite's allowance. Ends silently into Free. |
| **Lite** | $5/month or $50/year. The whole agent within the smallest allowance ($2.60 of AI a month, about 590 emails or 60 messages). The entry plan: Home with summaries and a few chats. |
| **Pro** | $10/month or $100/year via Stripe (`PRICE_USD`; a year is ten months). The whole agent within $5.80 a month (about 1,320 emails or 130 messages). |
| **Max** | $30/month or $300/year. The same agent within $18.50 a month (about 4,200 emails or 410 messages). |

**One allowance, never degrade.** Every paid plan has every feature and the same models; they differ only in `PLAN_ALLOWANCE_USD`: what the agent may cost per month, sized so a yearly plan keeps 20% after Stripe, the allowance spent and a busy inbox through Jev. Everything the agent does is paid from it at its real price (`charge`: model calls at `MODEL_PRICES`, reading aloud, transcription, OCR, autocomplete), so a feature the user never opens costs them nothing; there are no separate email, message, board or task limits. The user sees it as a share (`usedPercent`) and in round numbers of emails or messages (`allowanceIn`, from `TYPICAL_COST_USD`), never in dollars. Noise Jev settles is free. Once it is spent new mail is handled exactly as on Free (Jev, rules, spam shield) with no summary or draft, except urgent mail, which may run 10% over (`URGENT_RESERVE`); mail that needs the user still gets its Home card, unwritten; anything the user asks of the agent (chat, Ask, Telegram / WhatsApp texts and voice, reading aloud, a new board) is refused with the reset date (`requireBudget`), and background work (board entries and history, people notes, promises, the Reading summary, autocomplete, plain-language search) waits (`hasBudget`). It is monthly on yearly plans too (never a yearly pool) and resets on the 1st (UTC).

**Never push.** Paid plans are mentioned only in Settings → Plan (the usage meter and the plan choices), one ⌘K item ("Upgrade to Pro"), a muted "N days left" line in the sidebar during the trial, and a muted usage line once a month is ≥ 90% spent, and the upgrade dialog when a Free user opens a Pro feature. No modals that open on their own, no banners, no badges, no "Sent with Fewmail" signature.
