# Domain

Fewmail is an **email agent**: it reads every incoming email so the person does not have to, and shows them only what needs them. The UI is built around decisions, not around the inbox.

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Mailbox** | A Gmail account whose mail Fewmail reads. One is the account you sign in with; more can be added in Settings and their mail joins the same Home, queue and inbox; Mail can be narrowed to one of them. An added mailbox never signs anyone in to your account — signing in with it opens its own Fewmail. |
| **Home** | `/home` (`G H`): the live home screen. It updates on its own as mail arrives (no brief, no batches: a card appears as soon as the agent has read the email), and it is a few cards sorted by what matters, so a visit starts with what is important and has an end: **Needs you** first, then **Can wait**, Tidy, the Board and the newsletters. Each card says what it holds and opens over Home's column like Reading's; every email in it opens in the sidebar with what the agent proposes under it, so a card is never judged without its email. **What happened**: a header computed in the browser (no model text): the date, "The agent is reading 3 new emails" while a sync is under way, never during the first read, whose headline ("Reading your inbox…") and spinner line already say it: three spinners and three "reading"s read as stuck (`reading`: unanalysed inbox mail of the last day, counted only while a mailbox's `syncStartedAt` is set, so a failed analysis never reads forever; sync announces `today` before `processIncoming`), the headline counting what is important ("Seven things need you.", or "All done. Nothing needs you." once none is left), one serif sentence ("48 emails in the last day. Mostly newsletters and notifications, and just 5 from people. Your rules took care of 6.": the middle one only when fewer than half came from people, `pulse.people`; never "I read every one", which read as the agent boasting) and the calendar's events left today and tomorrow as chips linking to that day. The agent's **proposals** are one card after Can wait (below): every pending card that only moves mail (`PROPOSAL_KINDS`: archive, mark read, unsubscribe, spam, trash), including a rule's that asked instead of acting because the email looked important, and **Tidy** (below); whatever only moves mail stays apart, waiting for the yes, and is never counted as important: it is noise to clear when there is time. The headline and the sidebar's number (`today.count`, `countImportant`) count the same: each important card; the progress counts Quick and Can wait too, each in its own share. What rules did needs no yes (turning a rule on was it) and is not on Home: it is under Rules (see **Handled**). **Newsletters** closes the page: a card of the fresh issue's newsletters (see **Reading**), the count and the day, the first three by sender and subject, and **Mark all read** (`⇧I`); `N` (`?page=newsletters`) opens all of them over Home's column as Mail's rows (`J/K`, `⏎` in the sidebar, `E`, `⌫`), with a link to Reading's summary. Never the summary itself, which has its own page; hidden when none came (it is reading for later, so it never stands between the user and what is important). Fresh sign-in codes come first (see **Code**). **Needs you** (`Q`, `?page=look`) sits under the **Decisions** label and its progress bar ("2 of 11", what left Needs you, Can wait and Quick since the page opened, however it left, against what is left; each card its own share and color, and hovering a share names it: "Needs you: 2 of 7"; the headline and the sidebar still count the important cards only): one card saying what its cards ask by kind with the first three, whose pane holds every card that asks for a step (`isImportant`, `@fewmail/shared/agent`: every pending card but the proposals, and `read` and `heads_up` only at importance 3 or when a person waits for an answer the agent could not draft, Free or past the allowance, since a card that only asks to read is something to know, not to settle) as one-line rows. A row with an email opens it in the sidebar (`⏎`, `Space`, `O` or a click) with the card's **proposal** under it (`CardProposal`, `components/queue-card.tsx`, shown wherever that email opens, its page too): the draft or the step, the yes, **Skip** (`S`), with `⏎` / `⌘⏎` / `M` / `L`; once the card is settled the next card with an email takes the sidebar, so the list is worked through without coming back to it. A row without an email (an agent request, a clean-up) unfolds in place. The pane has batch picking: `X` picks the cursor card, `⌘A` picks all, then `⏎` approves, `S` skips and `⌫` deletes the emails of the picked cards; a skip or a delete ends in a toast with **Undo** (`actions.restore` puts a skipped card back, a deleted email returns to the inbox, and the progress counts it as waiting again). Once a card settles, a resting pointer clicks nothing on Home until it moves (`holdClicksUntilMove`): the next card rises under the pointer, and the second click of a double click would act on it; keys never wait. The cursor holds a card, not a position: a card arriving live above it never takes `⏎`. A card that came after the user's previous look at Home is marked new; a look is `today.open` (`openToday`): opening the page, or coming back to its tab after 10 minutes away, never a live refetch or the agent. Cards waiting more than 7 days are counted under the queue with **Skip them** (`⇧S`). A card made by the user's rule says so ("Your rule: …"), and an urgent one (importance 3) shows the red importance dot. A reply's proposal shows the draft under the email it answers and who the reply goes to; when its email names an exact meeting start it carries the event too and offers **Add to calendar** (`L`) beside Send, which adds it at once and leaves the reply waiting (`actions.addEvent`, `addCardEvent`), since Send never adds it; **Rewrite** takes how to rewrite it in words (empty: shorter); `R` edits the draft. The list is grouped by kind, as Mail's views group mail: each group headed by what its cards say (Send for replies, a consent's own verb) unless they differ, with its count, in the queue's order (the kind of the first card first; a group keeps its place while Home is open, so settling its first card never moves it below the next, and a new kind joins at the end), and every group but the last folded to its first five until Show all N (the cursor, picks and `⏎` work on what is shown). The agent's **consent** cards (see **Agent requests**) are important like any card that asks for a yes. **Can wait** (`W`, `?page=later`), counted by the progress in its own share, never by the headline: one card saying what it holds (its unread mail counted as "Worth knowing 2", never "important", a word Home keeps for Needs you), whose pane holds the cards that only ask to read (`read`, `heads_up`, opening in the sidebar the same way), Worth knowing's mail (below) and **new senders**. What the agent offers to take over has its own card, **The agent suggests** (`R`, `?page=improve`, gone when there is none): **suggested rules** (below) and **rule offers** (each untouched starter rule with a Gmail effect, `listRuleOffers`: Turn on or Keep off, either answer touches the rule so it is asked once); it counts them in its lead ("3 rules the agent could follow for you") and lists the first three. **Suggested rule** (`ruleSuggestions` / `declineRuleSuggestion` in `services/cleanup.ts`): a sender whose every analysed email of the last 30 days, at least three, the agent would archive or mark read (`analyses.suggestedAction`; an unsubscribe counts as archiving, since a rule keeps acting and leaving a list is a one-off), all below importance 2, none handled by a rule or kept by the user (a `dismissed` action), not covered by an enabled simple `from` rule and not one new senders already ask about; at most three. **Always archive** / **Always mark read** makes it a simple `from` rule (the user's standing yes); **No, thanks** keeps the sender's keyed hash (`declined_rule_suggestions`), so it is never offered again, on Home or in their mail. The same card stands above the sender's mail on its own page (`mail.thread`'s `ruleSuggestion`, under the thread's notices), and an answer there settles it on Home too. The agent sees them in `get_today` (`ruleSuggestions`) and may offer one with `create_rule`, never make one unasked. **New sender** (`newSenders` / `keepSender` in `services/cleanup.ts`): a mailing list whose first stored email is at most 14 days old, that wrote at least three times and was opened at most once, with no action on any of its emails, not left, not covered by an enabled `from` or `mailingList` simple rule; at most three. Its card asks once: **Always archive** (`A`, a simple `from` rule, the user's standing yes), **Unsubscribe** (`U`, confirmed first like Mailing lists) or **Keep as usual** (`I`, a `dismissed` action on its latest email, so it is never asked again). **Tidy**: quiet inbox mail (importance < 2) that no rule covers and the agent would archive, mark read or unsubscribe from (`analyses.suggestedAction`, `listTidy` / `settleTidy` in `services/mail.ts`): only a thread's newest email, never a thread with a pending card or an email already acted on. Jev's noise suggestion counts only when archive, mark read and unsubscribe together reach 0.7 of its odds (`NOISE_CONFIDENCE`), else the email has no suggestion and stays as it is; an unsubscribe is offered only for a sender the user plainly ignores (a `List-Unsubscribe` header, three emails or more, at most one opened), else as an archive, since leaving a list cannot be undone. What Tidy holds never shows under Worth knowing too. Nothing moves until the user approves. Tidy and the proposal cards share one card after Can wait ("The agent suggests tidying 23 emails", per kind, "Nothing changes in your mail until you approve"); **Approve all** (`T`, uk "Погодити") does them all in one yes (a confirmation first when that would leave a mailing list; the toast's Undo brings archives back to the inbox and takes read marks off, `mail.markUnread`), **Review** (`D`, uk "Подивитись", `/home?page=tidy`) takes Home's column with each email grouped by what the agent would do, with why: `J/K` walk, `⏎` approves one, `S` **Keep**s it as it is (a `dismissed` action, so it never comes back), `T` approves what is left, and Home comes back once none is left; what was done lands as a `done` action beside its email; an email Gmail refused stays in Tidy, counted as `failed` (the toast says so and its row comes back). The agent sees the same list in `get_today` (`tidy`) and carries it out with `tidy_mail` after a yes. Mail analyzed before the column existed has no suggestion. **Worth knowing**, needing no decision, is Can wait's mail, a group in its pane that Home's cursor walks after the cards with `⏎` / `E` / `⌫`: the other unread inbox mail of importance ≥ 1 from the catch-up window (only a conversation's newest email, never one a card was ever made for, settled or not, nor a thread with a pending card: one conversation sits in one block, and a skipped card never comes back as mail), mail of the last 7 days naming a due date or meeting time that no card or code shows (only a thread's newest dated email, so a moved meeting leaves its old time behind; dates as written; an ISO due date sorts soonest first and leaves once passed) and sent mail still awaiting an answer (named as the recipient's own mail signs, else the address); an email with a code is never there, since a code has its own block while fresh and means nothing after. Every one of its rows opens its email on its own page (`/mail/$threadId`). **Catch-up window** (`catchUp`, `services/home.ts`): Home covers everything since the user's last day on Home (`user_settings.todaySince`, moved to the previous visit on the first look on a new local day; the agent's `get_today` and `today.get` never count as a visit), at least the last 24 h and at most 7 days, so a few days away are caught up in one sitting; the header sentence says "in the last 3 days". The queue is never windowed. Empty sections hide. An email opened from a card (`O`) opens on its own page (`/mail/$threadId`); `/home?thread=` (agent answers, older links) redirects there. Internal names stay `today.*` (oRPC) and `get_today` (MCP clients depend on it). **Every block of Home is a card that opens** over Home's column, notices included: Needs you (`Q`), Can wait (`W`), Tidy (`D`), the Board (`B`) and Newsletters (`N`); a list of cards on Home itself, unfolding in place, left people clicking without knowing what would open, and a card's draft without its email read as nothing in particular. |
| **Queue** | Pending **actions** (`actions.status = 'pending'`), ordered by importance, then mail someone waits for an answer to, then age. Each is approved (`⏎`), edited, redrafted or skipped; `⇧⏎` approves every pending card of the selected one's kind at once (`approveCards`: one by one, each send with its own undo window). Batch approval is the answer to repetitive replies: nothing is ever sent without a yes. Telegram and WhatsApp end the queue with one button per kind that has two or more cards (`all:<kind>`). |
| **Action** | Something the agent proposes or did: `reply` (with a draft), `pay`, `schedule`, `follow_up`, `read`, `archive`, `mark_read`, `unsubscribe`, `spam`, `security`, `commitment`, `heads_up` (see **Heads-up**), `cleanup`, `consent` (an agent tool waiting for a yes, see **Agent requests**). A card's button never promises what Fewmail does not do. A `pay` or `read` card can carry the email's own link (`payload.link`: pay, sign, renew, confirm or read, shown with its host): ⏎ opens it in a new tab and the yes moves to `M`, saying what the user did ("I paid it", `actionDone`). A `schedule` card whose meeting has an exact start (`payload.event`) is approved as Add to calendar, which creates the event in the email's mailbox calendar; a `reply` card carries the same event for its own Add to calendar button and Send never adds it. ⏎ on a `follow_up` card writes the reminder, turning it into a `reply` card that still needs its own yes to send. Elsewhere the yes on those kinds only marks the email read and closes the card, and says so ("I scheduled it"). Statuses: `pending`, `done`, `dismissed` (skipped), `auto_done` (by an autonomous rule), `failed`. An email with a **code** never gets a card; the thread view shows each settled card (done, auto_done, failed) beside its own email, never as a list above the thread; pending ones live on Home. |
| **Handled** | What the agent did: `done` (after the user's yes), `auto_done` (by a rule the user turned on, or a sender they reported) and `failed`. Always reviewable, never hidden: the **journal** ("Done in your mail") sits under the rules on `/rules`, since rules do most of it, and lists the last 7 days by day, five per day until **Show all N**, with the email, what was done (a consent's yes is recorded as what its tool did, `recordDone`: a Gmail move becomes that kind, so it reads like a rule's and gets its undo), why, who allowed it ("You said yes" or "Your rule: …"), its time and an undo where one exists (archive, spam; it shows on hover over the time and leaves once used), failures first; the agent reads the same with `list_handled`. Without a yes or a rule the agent only reads. |
| **Time saved** | `timeSaved` (`services/mail.ts`): handled mail × 15 s plus replies sent from the agent's drafts × 3 min (`SECONDS_SAVED`), over the last 7 days. A muted line on Home whose tooltip gives both counts, `saved` in the agent's `get_today`, and on Mondays the **weekly report**: its own message in Telegram and WhatsApp at the morning hour (`weekReply`; outside WhatsApp's 24 h window the template's button `week` fetches it), nothing when no time was saved. Never inflated: only those two counts. |
| **Analysis** | Per-message result (Jev decides; on Pro the fast model writes the text, on Free the summary is the snippet): category, importance 0–3, one-line summary, reason, `needsReply`, extracted amount (a plain number and its ISO code, `1250.00 USD`, so the app formats it in the UI language; older free-text amounts show as written) / due date / meeting time, matched rule. When the LLM writes the text of mail that needs the user (importance ≥ 2), a bill or a ticket (`finance`, `travel`; `worthReading`), the email's PDFs and images (≤ 2, ≤ 10 MB, first 3 pages, `services/attachments.ts`, Mistral OCR) are read into its prompt and never stored. |
| **Importance** | 3 urgent: security, money overdue or due within two days, a service about to be suspended (a Google Cloud bill past due), a person blocked on the user (pushed to Telegram and WhatsApp immediately, each switchable in Settings → Notifications), 2 needs you soon, 1 worth knowing, 0 noise. |
| **Rule** | A plain-language instruction ("Newsletters and digests") + an effect. Gmail effects (`archive`, `mark_read`, `unsubscribe`, `trash`) act on their own and land under handled, except on mail that needs the user (importance ≥ 2: a failed build, a deadline): there the effect waits as a card on Home for a yes. Simple rules always act; `draft_reply` leaves a draft on Home, `always_show` pins the email to Home. A rule is on or off (`enabled`); adding one that matches an existing rule's words updates that rule instead of adding a twin. Rules list newest first in a fixed order; simple rules apply the first match in that order. New users get the same starter rules; those with a Gmail effect start switched off, since nobody said yes to them yet, and act only once the user turns them on, from Home's rule offers or Rules; beside them Home and a sender's mail suggest a simple rule for a sender the agent keeps judging quiet (see **Home** → Suggested rule) (a rule the user turned on or kept is their standing yes). On Free, only the Gmail effects. |
| **Language** | One setting for the whole product, chosen at onboarding (defaults to the browser's): the app UI, Telegram bot, summaries, reasons, action labels and starter rules use it. **Replies always use the language of the email being answered.** The public site follows the visitor's browser language, switchable in its footer, and the choice carries into onboarding. Legal documents stay in English (the binding version) with a translated note. |
| **Voice** | How drafts sound: learned from the user's recent sent mail (synced alongside the inbox) plus an optional `voice` note. |
| **Saved draft** | An unfinished email kept as a Gmail draft, never in our database: the editor saves it on its own a second after each change (no Save button; Discard deletes it), the agent's `create_draft`, or Gmail itself. Listed under Mail → Drafts, reopened in the editor; sending it from Fewmail or discarding it deletes it. Not the agent's reply drafts on Home. Free. |
| **Attachment** | A file of an email. The reader lists an email's files under it (an image shows its thumbnail) and opens each in a new tab, or downloads it when a browser cannot show it; images the email carries inside its body are drawn there, never listed twice. In spam an email loads nothing from the web (no image, pixel or font; a CSP in its frame) until **Show images**, a hover never warms its images, its files show no thumbnail and open only after a confirmation. The composer attaches files from the paperclip (`⇧⌘U`), a drop or a paste, up to 18 MB together; they are saved with the draft, survive Undo, and Forward carries the original's files. Free. |
| **Calendar** | `/calendar` (`G L`): the primary Google calendar of every mailbox, read live and never stored, as a day, week, month or year view. Events are added (`N`, or a press on empty time), edited and deleted there; saving or deleting a recurring event asks what it reaches: this event, this and following events, or all events; the agent does the same through `list_events` / `create_event` / `update_event` / `delete_event`, each change waiting for the user's yes. Free. |
| **Mail views** | Layouts of the same inbox in Mail's view picker (`?filter=`, remembered per browser), never slices of it: **Focused** (the default) groups it by what the user does with it (Important, People, Calendar and travel, Money, Notifications, Newsletters, Promotions, Earlier); **Unread** groups unread mail that way and puts the rest under Read; **Categories** groups it by the agent's analysis category, mail that needs the user first (Security with every risk-flagged email, Person, Work, Finance, Receipt, Calendar, Travel, Notification, Newsletter, Promotion, then Other with mail never analysed), newest first inside each; **Inbox** is one list, newest first. Every grouped view folds a group to its newest five until Show all (never the last one, which pages on), offers Mark N read and opens on the pinned threads. Web only: the agent's `list_mail` returns each email's category and filters by it, so it needs no layout of its own. Free. |
| **Folders** | A branch under Mail in the sidebar, open while Mail is or a saved draft is being edited: Snoozed, Sent, Drafts, Archive, Spam and Trash open those folders, each its own page (`/mail/sent`, `/mail/spam`…, `components/mail-page.tsx` behind thin routes; an old `/mail?filter=spam` redirects there) under its name, while the Mail view picker and `[ / ]` hold only the inbox views; **Sent** lists what the user sent, newest first, a row nobody answered named by whom it went to ("To anna@…"; recipients come with `list_mail` for `sent` only), and Drafts carries its count (`mail.draftCount`: Gmail's own total plus failed sends, never every draft read), then the user's own Gmail labels (`mail.labels`, `listUserLabels` in `services/labels.ts`: read live from every mailbox, merged by name, never stored), each opening the Mail search for it (`label:name` in Gmail's own form). Free. |
| **Pin** | Keep a thread in sight: a pin is Gmail's star (`STARRED` on the thread, so starring in Gmail pins too and the other way round). Every inbox view opens on the inbox's pinned threads, one line each and only the first three until "Show all", and the rest of the list leaves them out (`listMail` filter `pinned`, `isPinned` on each row; the agent's `list_mail` takes the same filter). `S` pins or unpins the cursor row or the open thread. Mail rows pick for a batch (`X`, `⌘A`, a checkbox over the unread dot): archive, mark read (`⇧I`, also the cursor row alone), snooze, pin, trash or report spam all of them at once, one toast whose Undo takes them all back. Archiving takes a thread off the pinned list but keeps the star. Free. |
| **Snooze** | Hide a thread until a time; it comes back to the inbox unread. Snoozed also lists what the user snoozed in Gmail itself (no wake time is known for those; Gmail brings them back, and Unsnooze ends Gmail's snooze too). The menu offers Later today, Tomorrow, This weekend and Next week, each only while it is true (`SNOOZE_PRESETS`: no Later today past 21:00, no This weekend on a weekend), and Pick date & time (one `SnoozePicker` for every snooze menu). Free. |
| **Follow-up** | "Remind me if no reply in N days" on a sent email (Free: the thread resurfaces). The agent also sets follow-ups itself on sent mail that expects an answer (all plans); Pro adds a nudge on Home. |
| **Knowledge** | Free-form facts the user gives the agent (prices, availability…), used in drafts and autocomplete. Pro. |
| **Spam shield** | Community spam protection: "Report spam" (`!`) moves the thread to Gmail spam and votes; enough independent votes flag that sender's mail for everyone. Only a sender the user reported themselves goes to spam on its own (that report was their yes); everything else the shield catches (community votes, spam lists, patterns, two or more weak signals) stays in the inbox marked "Looks like spam" with a pending `spam` card on Home, and its yes moves it and votes (`proposeSpam` / `approveAction`). Votes also count against the campaign's patterns — the same text, the sending network, the linked domains — so a spammer gains nothing by switching address. "Not spam" votes the other way. Free. |
| **Maybe spam** | One weak signal (odd domain name, freshly registered domain, weak sender authentication): labelled "Looks like spam" and kept out of Important, never moved. Two or more → a `spam` card waiting for a yes (see **Spam shield**). Free. |
| **Snippets** | Saved texts inserted with `;name` in the editor. Free. |
| **Agent labels** | Optional `Fewmail/<Category>` Gmail labels so the sorting shows in any Gmail client. Free. |
| **Mailing lists** | `/subscriptions` (`G U`; internal names stay `subscriptions`), uk «Розсилки», never "Subscriptions": people read it as paid subscriptions, and its lead never mentions spam, which read as the page moving mail to spam. Every mailing list that wrote in the last 30 days and the user has not left, busiest first, with how many emails came and how many were opened (`isOpened`: Fewmail's own archive, which takes UNREAD off with the inbox and is remembered as `readByArchive`, and a rule's archive or mark-read are not opens; the same rule counts opens for Clear-out, new senders and Tidy's unsubscribe evidence). A row's name (`⏎` / `O`) opens those emails in Mail (`listSearch`, `in:anywhere`, since the count includes trashed and spam mail that Gmail's search leaves out). Unsubscribe (`U`) asks first (leaving a list has no undo), keeps the row spinning until the answer and leaves the list the way a person would: RFC 8058 one-click, else its unsubscribe page (opened from our server, a one-click form on it sent, success only when the page says so: `lib/unsubscribe-page.ts`), else its `mailto:` (a request sent from the mailbox the list wrote to; part of the unsubscribe, so it needs no other approval); a page that needs a person comes back as `link` and the user finishes there (a toast in the web, the link in the agent's answer) and archives everything that list sent; only an unsubscribe that went out makes later mail a zombie. A sender the network knows ignores unsubscribes (and without a List-Id) comes back as `ignoresUnsubscribe`: the web asks whether to block it (`offerBlock`, a trash rule for the sender), the agent offers `create_rule`; nothing blocks it unasked. A list is its titled List-Id (each GitHub repository is its own row, named by the List-Id's title when the sender sends several), else its sender. A list enough Fewmail users get (10, `MIN_READERS`) shows the share of its readers who left it ("62% of its readers on Fewmail left it", `leftShare`) on its row and in Home's new senders: `list_sightings` keeps keyed hashes of the list and the user, never more. Free. |
| **Clear-out** | Moving the whole inbox backlog out at once (`scanClearOut` / `runClearOut` / `undoClearOut` in `services/cleanup.ts`). Groups (`CLEAR_OUT_GROUPS`): ads and social older than 3 days, read notifications older than 30 days, mailing lists with 3+ emails none opened (found in the newest 600 inbox emails' headers; also unsubscribed: the run leaves exactly the lists the scan showed, passed back as `lists`, never ones it finds itself), everything older than a year (off by default). Read live from Gmail, headers only, nothing stored; up to 50,000 emails per mailbox. Each run is archived under its own label, `Fewmail/Cleared/<UTC stamp>` (`run`), so Undo brings back that run only and drops its label (unsubscribes stay); the agent's `undo_clear_out` without a run takes the latest. A mailbox Gmail refused does not stop the others: the result says `failed` and the toast still offers Undo for what moved. It starts from the sidebar's **Clear out the inbox** row above the account menu, only on a press (never on its own: right after onboarding it fought the first sync for Gmail's quota): the scan runs under a loading toast, never in an open dialog, and the toast's answer is the count that can go with **Review**, which opens the dialog on that same scan (or says there is nothing to clear); the row spins while the scan runs and, within 5 minutes of it, opens the dialog at once (`openClearOut`); the agent has `preview_clear_out`, `clear_out` and `undo_clear_out`. Free. |
| **Wrapped** | `/wrapped` (`G W`, the sidebar row after Mailing lists, uk «Підсумки»): the year or month so far (`Y` / `M`) in counts: mail in (from people, from lists and the rest, so the parts add up; people is by sender: all a sender wrote who wrote at least one personal email, except mailing-list mail, so their invoice counts too), handled (`handledSince(…, true)`: what rules and reported senders did plus the Gmail moves the user said yes to — Tidy, a card, a consent; Home's "your rules took care of" counts without them), with a muted hint under the card linking Rules while it is 0 (nothing changes in Gmail without a yes, so a new account starts there), replies sent from drafts, time saved, lists left, spam stopped, the busiest weekday and, separately, the busiest hour (shown as a range, 23:00–00:00), plus the three people who wrote most, counted the same way (in the app only). `getWrapped` in `services/wrapped.ts`; the agent's `get_wrapped`. Shared as a link `/w/<token>` whose token carries the counts signed with HMAC (no table, nothing stored, never a name or address) and as a 1080×1350 PNG drawn in the browser (`I`). Link previews: Caddy sends social crawlers on `/w/*` to the API's `/og/w/<token>` (Open Graph tags) whose image `/og/w/<token>/image.png` is drawn on the server (`services/wrapped-image.ts`, resvg-wasm, Inter and Lora from Google Fonts; the token carries the sharer's language, scripts those fonts lack fall back to English). Free. |
| **Reading** | A page of its own (`/reading`, `G N`, the sidebar row under Mail), kept as **daily issues**: one calendar day of newsletters (category `newsletter`, wherever they are; the starter rule archives them) in the user's time zone, written once the morning after and stored for good (`reading_issues`, one row per user and day: the newsletters it covers and the agent's summary, encrypted). The page opens on the latest issue: the summary, then every newsletter it was written from; the day still filling is never shown. Home keeps only the **fresh issue**'s newsletters (written in the last 24 h, so yesterday's from the morning hour until the next) as its Newsletters card, never the summary. Under the issue, **Every issue** lists the others newest first; a row, a digest's or a notification's link open that day (`/reading?day=YYYY-MM-DD`; `/home?day=` in digests already sent redirects there), and `esc` comes back to the latest. With nothing to read (no issue yet, or every newsletter of one trashed) the page says so and what will come: each morning the agent writes the news from the newsletters there, each line linked to its email. With the agent an issue is **one summary of all its newsletters** (`writeReadingIssue`): an intro, up to four topics whose sentences end on the newsletters they came from (a badge that opens that newsletter), where several cover one thing said once with where they disagree, then up to two worth reading in full with why; promotions and newsletters with nothing new are left out and counted. It is written by one fast-model call from each newsletter's own summary (never the bodies), keeping their facts and numbers as written: it never adds a detail, never says one is missing and never pads a topic with a sentence that states no fact. The model's own source numbers ("[2]", "(n2)") and a topic title repeated at the start of its sentence are stripped when the summary is read (`withoutEchoes`). ▶ in an issue's head reads its summary aloud (`/voice/reading?day=`), folded or open.
