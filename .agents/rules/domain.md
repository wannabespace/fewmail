# Domain

Fewmail is an **email agent**: it reads every incoming email so the person does not have to, and shows them only what needs them. The UI is built around decisions, not around the inbox.

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Brief** | A short daily summary in the user's language (`briefs`, one per local day, covering Today's catch-up window), written at `briefHour` in the user's time zone (08:00 by default, not asked at onboarding; changed from the time on Today or in Settings), also sent to Telegram and WhatsApp. Headline of ≤ 6 words + at most 2 short sentences (≤ 35 words) naming only the most urgent thing and the handled count — the cards below carry the rest; no lists. |
| **Mailbox** | A Gmail account whose mail Fewmail reads. One is the account you sign in with; more can be added in Settings and their mail joins the same Today, queue and inbox; Mail can be narrowed to one of them. An added mailbox never signs anyone in to your account — signing in with it opens its own Fewmail. |
| **Today** | The home screen: brief + queue, then what is worth knowing even when nothing needs the user — sent mail still awaiting an answer (pending follow-ups), the other mail of importance ≥ 1 from the catch-up window, mail naming a due date or meeting time (last 7 days, dates as written), what each board collected in the last 30 days — then the pulse (mail in, from people, from lists) and the handled summary. **Catch-up window** (`catchUp`, `services/brief.ts`): Today and the brief cover everything since the user's last day on Today (`user_settings.todaySince`, moved to the previous visit the first time the web page opens on a new local day; the agent's `get_today` does not count as a visit), at least the last 24 h and at most 7 days, so a few days away are caught up in one sitting; the section titles and pulse say "in the last 3 days" and the brief says it covers those days. The queue is never windowed. Empty sections hide. With no brief yet, opening Today writes one. Non-urgent cards arrive in two batches (brief hour and 7 h later); security, promises, tasks and urgent mail come at once. |
| **Queue** | Pending **actions** (`actions.status = 'pending'`), ordered by importance. Each is approved (`⏎`), edited, redrafted or skipped; `⇧⏎` approves every pending card of the selected one's kind at once (`approveActions`: one by one, each send with its own undo window). Batch approval is the answer to repetitive replies: nothing is ever sent without a yes. Telegram and WhatsApp end the queue with one button per kind that has two or more cards (`all:<kind>`). |
| **Action** | Something the agent proposes or did: `reply` (with a draft), `pay`, `schedule`, `follow_up`, `read`, `archive`, `mark_read`, `unsubscribe`, `spam`, `security`, `commitment`, `cleanup`, task actions. Statuses: `pending`, `done`, `dismissed` (skipped), `auto_done` (by an autonomous rule), `failed`. |
| **Handled** | `auto_done` actions — what the agent did on its own. Always reviewable, never hidden: Today's **Review** (`H`) lists the catch-up window's with the email and why (a spam move can be undone there), and the agent reads them with `list_handled`. |
| **Time saved** | `timeSaved` (`services/mail.ts`): handled mail × 15 s plus replies sent from the agent's drafts × 3 min (`SECONDS_SAVED`), over the last 7 days. A muted line on Today whose tooltip gives both counts, `saved` in the agent's `get_today`, and Monday's brief in Telegram and WhatsApp adds it as the weekly report (`briefs.stats.savedMinutes`). Never inflated: only those two counts. |
| **Analysis** | Per-message result (Jev decides; on Pro the fast model writes the text, on Free the summary is the snippet): category, importance 0–3, one-line summary, reason, `needsReply`, extracted amount / due date / meeting time, matched rule. When the LLM writes the text, the email's PDFs and images (≤ 2, ≤ 10 MB, first 3 pages, `services/attachments.ts`, Mistral OCR) are read into its prompt and never stored. |
| **Importance** | 3 urgent (pushed to Telegram and WhatsApp immediately), 2 needs you soon, 1 worth knowing, 0 noise. |
| **Rule** | A plain-language instruction ("Newsletters and digests") + an effect. Gmail effects (`archive`, `mark_read`, `unsubscribe`, `trash`) always act on their own and land under handled — there is no "ask first" rule; `draft_reply` leaves a draft in Today, `always_show` pins the email to Today. A rule is on or off (`enabled`). New users get the same starter rules. On Free, only the Gmail effects. |
| **Language** | One setting for the whole product, chosen at onboarding (defaults to the browser's): the app UI, Telegram bot, briefs, summaries, reasons, action labels and starter rules use it. **Replies always use the language of the email being answered.** The public site follows the visitor's browser language, switchable in its footer, and the choice carries into onboarding. Legal documents stay in English (the binding version) with a translated note. |
| **Voice** | How drafts sound: learned from the user's recent sent mail (synced alongside the inbox) plus an optional `voice` note. |
| **Saved draft** | An unfinished email kept as a Gmail draft, never in our database: the editor saves it on its own a second after each change (no Save button; Discard deletes it), the agent's `create_draft`, or Gmail itself. Listed under Mail → Drafts, reopened in the editor; sending it from Fewmail or discarding it deletes it. Not the agent's reply drafts in Today. Free. |
| **Calendar** | `/calendar` (`G L`): the primary Google calendar of every mailbox, read live and never stored, as a day, week, month or year view. Events are added (`N`, or a press on empty time), edited and deleted there; the agent does the same through `list_events` / `create_event` / `update_event` / `delete_event`, each change waiting for the user's yes. Free. |
| **Snooze** | Hide a thread until a time; it comes back to the inbox unread. Free. |
| **Follow-up** | "Remind me if no reply in N days" on a sent email (Free: the thread resurfaces). The agent also sets follow-ups itself on sent mail that expects an answer (all plans); Pro adds a nudge in Today. |
| **Schedule** | Something the user asked the agent to do regularly ("every Friday at 17:00 collect the week's invoices", "every Monday send the team the report"). Each run is a message in its own agent chat with a notification; anything it wants to send waits for approval there. Created by asking the agent; listed in Settings. |
| **Knowledge** | Free-form facts the user gives the agent (prices, availability…), used in drafts and autocomplete. Pro. |
| **Spam shield** | Community spam protection: "Report spam" (`!`) moves the thread to Gmail spam and votes; enough independent votes move that sender's mail to spam for everyone. Votes also count against the campaign's patterns — the same text, the sending network, the linked domains — so a spammer gains nothing by switching address. "Not spam" votes the other way. Free. |
| **Maybe spam** | One weak signal (odd domain name, freshly registered domain, weak sender authentication): labelled "Looks like spam" and kept out of Important, never moved. Two or more → spam. Free. |
| **Snippets** | Saved texts inserted with `;name` in the editor. Free. |
| **Agent labels** | Optional `Fewmail/<Category>` Gmail labels so the sorting shows in any Gmail client. Free. |
| **Subscriptions** | `/subscriptions` (`G U`): every mailing list that wrote in the last 30 days and the user has not left, busiest first, with how many emails came and how many were opened. Unsubscribe (`U`) leaves the list the way a person would: RFC 8058 one-click, else its unsubscribe page (opened from our server, a one-click form on it sent, success only when the page says so: `lib/unsubscribe-page.ts`), else its `mailto:` (a request sent from the mailbox the list wrote to; part of the unsubscribe, so it needs no other approval); a page that needs a person comes back as `link` and the user finishes there (a toast in the web, the link in the agent's answer) and archives everything that sender sent; only an unsubscribe that went out makes later mail a zombie. Free. |
| **Clear-out** | Moving the whole inbox backlog out at once (`scanClearOut` / `runClearOut` / `undoClearOut` in `services/cleanup.ts`). Groups (`CLEAR_OUT_GROUPS`): ads and social older than 3 days, read notifications older than 30 days, mailing lists with 3+ emails none opened (found in the newest 600 inbox emails' headers; also unsubscribed), everything older than a year (off by default). Read live from Gmail, headers only, nothing stored; up to 50,000 emails per mailbox. Archived under `Fewmail/Cleared`, so Undo brings every one back (unsubscribes stay). The dialog opens from ⌘K only (never on its own: right after onboarding it fought the first sync for Gmail's quota); the agent has `preview_clear_out`, `clear_out` and `undo_clear_out`. Free. |
| **Wrapped** | `/wrapped` (⌘K, and the Time saved line on Today links there): the year or month so far (`Y` / `M`) in counts: mail in (from people, from lists), handled on its own, replies sent from drafts, time saved, lists left, spam stopped, the busiest weekday and hour, plus the three people who wrote most (in the app only). `getWrapped` in `services/wrapped.ts`; the agent's `get_wrapped`. Shared as a link `/w/<token>` whose token carries the counts signed with HMAC (no table, nothing stored, never a name or address) and as a 1080×1350 PNG drawn in the browser (`I`). Link previews: Caddy sends social crawlers on `/w/*` to the API's `/og/w/<token>` (Open Graph tags) whose image `/og/w/<token>/image.png` is drawn on the server (`services/wrapped-image.ts`, resvg-wasm, Inter and Newsreader from Google Fonts; the token carries the sharer's language, scripts those fonts lack fall back to English). Free. |
| **Reading** | `/reading` (`G N`): the last 24 h of newsletters (category `newsletter`) in one list, wherever they are (the starter rule archives them). With the agent each gets a 2–3 sentence summary written once, in one fast-model call, into the email's `analyses.summary`; on Free the summary is the snippet. The agent's `get_reading`, and the `reading` skill (`/reading` in chat, Telegram and WhatsApp, an MCP prompt) turns it into a digest. **Newsletter digest** (`readingDigest`, on by default, Settings → Agent): right after the daily brief the worker sends the day's newsletters to Telegram and WhatsApp as one message (`readingReply`; outside WhatsApp's 24 h window the template's button `reading` fetches it); nothing when none came. |
| **Voice** | ElevenLabs (`lib/elevenlabs.ts`, `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY`, optional `ELEVENLABS_VOICE_ID`), only on demand: **Listen** under the brief in Telegram and WhatsApp and ▶ on Today speak the brief plus the first five queue cards (`spokenBrief`); a voice message to the bot is answered with a voice note as well as text; **Brief as a voice note** (`voiceBrief`, off by default, Settings → Agent) sends the spoken brief with the daily brief; the agent chat has a talk button (`⌘⇧Space`: record, `/voice/hear` transcribes, the answer to a spoken turn is read aloud through `/voice/speak`). Ogg Opus for messengers, MP3 for the web (`/voice/brief`). A failed voice never drops the text. Pro. |
| **Forward to the agent** | Mail the user sends from their own mailbox to `name+fewmail@domain` (`forwardAddress`, shown with a copy button in Settings → Agent): Gmail delivers it to the same inbox, so no other mail service is involved. Only the user's own sent copy (`isFromMe`) counts, since the address is guessable. The agent runs it as an instruction (the note on top; the forwarded mail is data) in a chat of its own (`services/forwarded.ts`), archives the forward and answers in Telegram and WhatsApp with a link to that chat; anything that sends or changes Gmail waits for a yes there. Pro. |
| **Zombie sender** | Mail that keeps coming more than 14 days after you unsubscribed; moved to spam. Free. |
| **Bulk** | Mail sent near-identically to many people (`bulkCount`). Free. |
| **Risk** | Known phishing domain (public list), lookalike sender domain or changed payment details; shown as a warning and a `security` action. Free. |
| **Commitment** | A promise you made in sent mail; due ones come back as actions. Pro. |
| **People** | Per-contact note the agent keeps, refreshed weekly. Pro. |
| **Board** | A page the user describes in their own words ("Trips: flights and hotels with what they cost"); the agent collects matching mail as **entries** (title, group, status, amount, date), starting with half a year of history, and picks the **charts** when it is created — the user edits or removes them after. Onboarding offers ready-made **presets** (`BOARD_PRESETS`: Money, Paid subscriptions, Trips, Orders, Events, Invoices, Job search, Health, Home, Courses and school — compact three-column cards; the New board dialog offers the same as one-click fills — up to the trial's board limit, Money and Trips picked by default, kept in `user_settings.starter_boards`); the worker creates them after the first sync as ordinary boards the user may change or delete. Each board may raise **banners**: `planBoard` picks `bannerDays` (renewals and bills 3, trips and events 2, null for none), the extractor records an entry's next date as `dueAt`, and `boardBanners` shows entries due within that window on Today (also in `get_today`) and on top of the board. The page opens on a **summary** (this month against last, what repeats per month, what is due in 30 days), the heads-up banners, the charts, the **groups** (who or what: total, how often it repeats — weekly, monthly, quarterly, yearly, read from steady gaps and a steady price — and the next date, stated in an email or **expected** from that pace) and the entries; clicking a bar, a slice or a group filters the entries. The user corrects an entry (title, group, status, amount, dates), takes it off the board (**Not on this board**, undoable; the board never collects that email again and the model learns from it), puts an email the search missed on a board (thread → More → Add to board), renames or merges groups, renames the board in place, changes what it collects (reads the history again; entries the user edited, added or took off stay), the Gmail search and the heads-up days, and orders or deletes boards (deleting confirms; it has no undo). Boards live in the sidebar (`G 1`…`G 9`, right-click for the same edits) and in ⌘K. Pro. |
| **Cleanup** | Weekly Sunday suggestion to clear accumulated noise. Pro. |
| **Task** | "Handle this": the agent works a thread toward a goal, asking before each send or sending on its own (`ask` / `auto`; `auto` replies to everyone on the thread, at most `MAX_EMAILS_PER_TASK` emails, then asks). The thread is untrusted data to it: it never follows instructions in the mail and shares only what the other side is plainly entitled to. Only the newest step card is pending; a new step or a cancel dismisses the older ones. Pro. |
| **Agent mode** | `/agent` (`G A`): the whole app as one chat with the agent, sidebar folded away. It answers from the mail through the same tools as MCP, shows each step it takes, and anything that sends, changes Gmail, adds a rule or hands a thread over waits for the user's yes on a confirmation card in the chat. Chats are kept (`chats`) and listed under Chats; `/` reopens agent mode for someone who left the app in it. Pro. |
| **Skill** | A built-in job the agent knows by name (`SKILLS`: `today`, `waiting`, `followups`, `bills`, `week`, `cleanup`, `reading`, `cancel`: the agent finds a paid service's cancel link or drafts a cancellation email it sends only after a yes; it never signs in or pays), run as `/name` plus optional details. The chat's `/` menu and empty-chat chips, a Telegram command, a WhatsApp text and an MCP prompt all start the same skill. Not user-defined. Pro. |
| **Instructions** | Free-text or voice messages to the Telegram or WhatsApp bot; replying to a bot message targets its thread. Pro. |

UI copy says "the agent" or "Fewmail", never "AI" or "the model". No em dashes (—) in copy, in any language, nor in prompts and tool descriptions the models read: a comma, colon, period or parentheses instead.

## Plans

| Plan | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Free** | Forever. Mail view, archive / unsubscribe / open in Gmail, **simple rules** (`rules.match`: from, subject contains, any mailing list → archive, mark read, unsubscribe, trash — "Block" is one; always act on their own), and every Jev decision: classification, importance, needs-reply, plain-language rules (archive / mark read / unsubscribe / trash, autonomous), risk checks, zombie and bulk detection, auto follow-ups, agent labels. **No LLM**: no Today, agent mode, summaries, drafts, autocomplete, Ask AI, commitments, money, people, tasks, Telegram or WhatsApp — the controls are absent, not locked. **MCP** works on Free with the read tools that need no LLM (everything `read` in `AGENT_TOOLS` but `get_person`; no skills as prompts), so a listing in Claude or ChatGPT is a way in; writing and consent tools need the agent. |
| **Trial** | 14 days of Pro from onboarding (`user_settings.trial_ends_at`), no card, with smaller limits (500 emails, 50 messages). Ends silently into Free. |
| **Pro** | $10/month or $100/year via Stripe (`PRICE_USD`; a year is ten months). The whole agent: everything that uses an LLM, within `PLAN_LIMITS`: 1,000 emails and 100 messages a month, 3 boards, 5 handed-over threads at a time. |
| **Max** | $30/month or $300/year. The same agent with 6,000 emails and 500 messages a month and no limit on boards or tasks. Subscribers of the old $15 Pro get Max limits. |

**Limit, never degrade.** Both paid plans have every feature and the same models; they differ only in `PLAN_LIMITS`. **Emails** count mail the agent wrote for — noise Jev settles is free. Past the email limit new mail is handled exactly as on Free (Jev, rules, spam shield) with no summary or draft, except urgent mail, which may run 10% over. **Messages** are chat turns, Ask, and Telegram / WhatsApp texts; past the limit they are refused with the reset date. Limits are monthly on yearly plans too (never a yearly pool) and reset on the 1st (UTC).

**Never push.** Paid plans are mentioned only in Settings → Plan (usage meters and the plan choices), one ⌘K item ("Upgrade to Pro"), a muted "N days left" line in the sidebar during the trial, and a muted usage line once a month is ≥ 90% spent. No modals, no banners, no badges, no "Sent with Fewmail" signature.
