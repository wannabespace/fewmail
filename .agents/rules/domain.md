# Domain

Fewmail is an **email agent**: it reads every incoming email so the person does not have to, and shows them only what needs them. The UI is built around decisions, not around the inbox.

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Mailbox** | A Gmail account whose mail Fewmail reads. One is the account you sign in with; more can be added in Settings and their mail joins the same Home, queue and inbox; Mail can be narrowed to one of them. An added mailbox never signs anyone in to your account — signing in with it opens its own Fewmail. |
| **Home** | `/home` (`G H`): the live home screen. It updates on its own as mail arrives (no brief, no batches: a card appears as soon as the agent has read the email), and it answers three questions top to bottom, so a visit has an end. **What happened**: a header computed in the browser (no model text): the date, "The agent is reading 3 new emails" while a sync is under way (`reading`: unanalysed inbox mail of the last day, counted only while a mailbox's `syncStartedAt` is set, so a failed analysis never reads forever; sync announces `today` before `processIncoming`), the headline counting the decisions ("Seven things need you.", or "All done. Nothing needs you." once none is left), one serif sentence ("48 emails in the last day. I read every one, and nothing changes in your mail without your yes. Your rules took care of 6.") and the calendar's events left today and tomorrow as chips linking to that day. The agent's **proposals** are one card at the top of Decisions (below): every pending card that only moves mail (`PROPOSAL_KINDS`: archive, mark read, unsubscribe, spam, trash), including a rule's that asked instead of acting because the email looked important, and **Tidy** (below); whatever only moves mail stays apart from the decisions, waiting for the yes and counted as one decision. The headline, the progress and the sidebar's number (`today.count`, `countToday`) count the same: each card, one for the proposals and Tidy together, each new sender and each rule offer; a board's heads-up asks nothing and is not counted. What rules did needs no yes (turning a rule on was it) and is not on Home: it is under Rules (see **Handled**). Then **What your newsletters said**: the opening of the Reading summary and its topic titles in serif, no card, with "12 newsletters today" (`N`) unfolding Reading in place; hidden when none came (it is always a few lines, so it sits above the decisions without pushing them away). **What needs me**: one **Decisions** list with a progress bar ("2 of 7", the decisions settled since the page opened against what is left): fresh sign-in codes first (see **Code**), then the proposals card (**Quick**: the cursor's first stop, `⏎` approves all of it; every card starts folded, a click on its header or `Space` unfolds it), then the queue without the proposals (**Needs a look**; the two labels show only when both are there), its first 5 cards and **Show all N** (`J` past the last or `⌘A` shows them too), with batch picking: `X` picks the cursor card, `⌘A` picks all, then `⏎` approves, `S` skips and `⌫` deletes the emails of the picked cards; a skip or a delete ends in a toast with **Undo** (`actions.restore` puts a skipped card back, a deleted email returns to the inbox, and the progress counts it as waiting again). Once a card settles, a resting pointer clicks nothing on Home until it moves (`holdClicksUntilMove`): the next card rises under the pointer, and the second click of a double click would act on it; keys never wait. The cursor holds a card, not a position: a card arriving live above it never takes `⏎`. A card that came after the user's previous look at Home is marked new; a look is `today.open` (`openToday`): opening the page, or coming back to its tab after 10 minutes away, never a live refetch or the agent. Cards waiting more than 7 days are counted under the queue with **Skip them** (`⇧S`). A card made by the user's rule says so ("Your rule: …"), and an urgent one (importance 3) shows the red importance dot. A reply card shows what the sender wrote (the snippet) and who the reply goes to around the draft; when its email names an exact meeting start it carries the event too and offers **Add to calendar** (`L`) beside Send, which adds it at once and leaves the reply waiting (`actions.addEvent`, `addCardEvent`), since Send never adds it; **Rewrite** (`R`) takes how to rewrite it in words (empty: shorter). With five or more cards of at least two kinds, pills above the list narrow it to one kind, each saying what its cards say (Send for replies, a consent's own verb) unless they differ (`F` cycles, "All" first; the cursor, picks and `⏎` work on what is shown). After the queue in the same list: the agent's **consent** cards (see **Agent requests**), **new senders**, **rule offers** (each untouched starter rule with a Gmail effect, `listRuleOffers`: Turn on or Keep off, either answer touches the rule so it is asked once), then the board heads-ups. **New sender** (`newSenders` / `keepSender` in `services/cleanup.ts`): a mailing list whose first stored email is at most 14 days old, that wrote at least three times and was opened at most once, with no action on any of its emails, not left, not covered by an enabled `from` or `mailingList` simple rule; at most three. Its card asks once: **Always archive** (`A`, a simple `from` rule, the user's standing yes), **Unsubscribe** (`U`, confirmed first like Subscriptions) or **Keep as usual** (`I`, a `dismissed` action on its latest email, so it is never asked again). **Tidy**: quiet inbox mail (importance < 2) that no rule covers and the agent would archive, mark read or unsubscribe from (`analyses.suggestedAction`, `listTidy` / `settleTidy` in `services/mail.ts`): only a thread's newest email, never a thread with a pending card or an email already acted on. Jev's noise suggestion counts only when archive, mark read and unsubscribe together reach 0.7 of its odds (`NOISE_CONFIDENCE`), else the email has no suggestion and stays as it is; an unsubscribe is offered only for a sender the user plainly ignores (a `List-Unsubscribe` header, three emails or more, at most one opened), else as an archive, since leaving a list cannot be undone. What Tidy holds never shows under Worth knowing too. Nothing moves until the user approves. Tidy and the proposal cards share one card at the top of Decisions ("The agent suggests tidying 23 emails", per kind, "Nothing changes in your mail until you approve"); **Approve all** (`T`, uk "Погодити") does them all in one yes (a confirmation first when that would leave a mailing list; the toast's Undo brings archives back to the inbox and takes read marks off, `mail.markUnread`), **Review** (`D`, uk "Подивитись", `/home?page=tidy`) takes Home's column with each email grouped by what the agent would do, with why: `J/K` walk, `⏎` approves one, `S` **Keep**s it as it is (a `dismissed` action, so it never comes back), `T` approves what is left, and Home comes back once none is left; what was done lands as a `done` action beside its email; an email Gmail refused stays in Tidy, counted as `failed` (the toast says so and its row comes back). The agent sees the same list in `get_today` (`tidy`) and carries it out with `tidy_mail` after a yes. Mail analyzed before the column existed has no suggestion. **What is worth knowing**, needing no decision: one **Worth knowing** list of the other unread inbox mail of importance ≥ 1 from the catch-up window, mail of the last 7 days naming a due date or meeting time that no card or code shows (only a thread's newest dated email, so a moved meeting leaves its old time behind; dates as written; an ISO due date sorts soonest first and leaves once passed) and sent mail still awaiting an answer (named as the recipient's own mail signs, else the address); an email with a code is never there, since a code has its own block while fresh and means nothing after. Boards are not on Home (the sidebar has them; `get_today` still returns their digest). Every row below the queue opens its email on its own page (`/mail/$threadId`). **Catch-up window** (`catchUp`, `services/home.ts`): Home covers everything since the user's last day on Home (`user_settings.todaySince`, moved to the previous visit on the first look on a new local day; the agent's `get_today` and `today.get` never count as a visit), at least the last 24 h and at most 7 days, so a few days away are caught up in one sitting; the header sentence says "in the last 3 days". The queue is never windowed. Empty sections hide. An email opened from a card (`O`) opens on its own page (`/mail/$threadId`); `/home?thread=` (agent answers, older links) redirects there. Internal names stay `today.*` (oRPC) and `get_today` (MCP clients depend on it). |
| **Queue** | Pending **actions** (`actions.status = 'pending'`), ordered by importance, then mail someone waits for an answer to, then age. Each is approved (`⏎`), edited, redrafted or skipped; `⇧⏎` approves every pending card of the selected one's kind at once (`approveActions`: one by one, each send with its own undo window). Batch approval is the answer to repetitive replies: nothing is ever sent without a yes. Telegram and WhatsApp end the queue with one button per kind that has two or more cards (`all:<kind>`). |
| **Action** | Something the agent proposes or did: `reply` (with a draft), `pay`, `schedule`, `follow_up`, `read`, `archive`, `mark_read`, `unsubscribe`, `spam`, `security`, `commitment`, `cleanup`, `consent` (an agent tool waiting for a yes, see **Agent requests**). A card's button never promises what Fewmail does not do. A `pay` or `read` card can carry the email's own link (`payload.link`: pay, sign, renew, confirm or read, shown with its host): ⏎ opens it in a new tab and the yes moves to `M`, saying what the user did ("I paid it", `actionDone`). A `schedule` card whose meeting has an exact start (`payload.event`) is approved as Add to calendar, which creates the event in the email's mailbox calendar; a `reply` card carries the same event for its own Add to calendar button and Send never adds it. ⏎ on a `follow_up` card writes the reminder, turning it into a `reply` card that still needs its own yes to send. Elsewhere the yes on those kinds only marks the email read and closes the card, and says so ("I scheduled it"). Statuses: `pending`, `done`, `dismissed` (skipped), `auto_done` (by an autonomous rule), `failed`. An email with a **code** never gets a card; the thread view shows each settled card (done, auto_done, failed) beside its own email, never as a list above the thread; pending ones live on Home. |
| **Handled** | What the agent did: `done` (after the user's yes), `auto_done` (by a rule the user turned on, or a sender they reported) and `failed`. Always reviewable, never hidden: the **journal** ("Done in your mail") sits under the rules on `/rules`, since rules do most of it, and lists the last 7 days by day, five per day until **Show all N**, with the email, what was done (a consent's yes is recorded as what its tool did, `recordDone`: a Gmail move becomes that kind, so it reads like a rule's and gets its undo), why, who allowed it ("You said yes" or "Your rule: …"), its time and an undo where one exists (archive, spam; it shows on hover over the time and leaves once used), failures first; the agent reads the same with `list_handled`. Without a yes or a rule the agent only reads. |
| **Time saved** | `timeSaved` (`services/mail.ts`): handled mail × 15 s plus replies sent from the agent's drafts × 3 min (`SECONDS_SAVED`), over the last 7 days. A muted line on Home whose tooltip gives both counts, `saved` in the agent's `get_today`, and on Mondays the **weekly report**: its own message in Telegram and WhatsApp at the morning hour (`weekReply`; outside WhatsApp's 24 h window the template's button `week` fetches it), nothing when no time was saved. Never inflated: only those two counts. |
| **Analysis** | Per-message result (Jev decides; on Pro the fast model writes the text, on Free the summary is the snippet): category, importance 0–3, one-line summary, reason, `needsReply`, extracted amount (a plain number and its ISO code, `1250.00 USD`, so the app formats it in the UI language; older free-text amounts show as written) / due date / meeting time, matched rule. When the LLM writes the text of mail that needs the user (importance ≥ 2), a bill or a ticket (`finance`, `travel`; `worthReading`), the email's PDFs and images (≤ 2, ≤ 10 MB, first 3 pages, `services/attachments.ts`, Mistral OCR) are read into its prompt and never stored. |
| **Importance** | 3 urgent (pushed to Telegram and WhatsApp immediately, each switchable in Settings → Notifications), 2 needs you soon, 1 worth knowing, 0 noise. |
| **Rule** | A plain-language instruction ("Newsletters and digests") + an effect. Gmail effects (`archive`, `mark_read`, `unsubscribe`, `trash`) act on their own and land under handled, except on mail that needs the user (importance ≥ 2: a failed build, a deadline): there the effect waits as a card on Home for a yes. Simple rules always act; `draft_reply` leaves a draft on Home, `always_show` pins the email to Home. A rule is on or off (`enabled`); adding one that matches an existing rule's words updates that rule instead of adding a twin. Rules list newest first in a fixed order; simple rules apply the first match in that order. New users get the same starter rules; those with a Gmail effect start switched off, since nobody said yes to them yet, and act only once the user turns them on, from Home's rule offers or Rules (a rule the user turned on or kept is their standing yes). On Free, only the Gmail effects. |
| **Language** | One setting for the whole product, chosen at onboarding (defaults to the browser's): the app UI, Telegram bot, summaries, reasons, action labels and starter rules use it. **Replies always use the language of the email being answered.** The public site follows the visitor's browser language, switchable in its footer, and the choice carries into onboarding. Legal documents stay in English (the binding version) with a translated note. |
| **Voice** | How drafts sound: learned from the user's recent sent mail (synced alongside the inbox) plus an optional `voice` note. |
| **Saved draft** | An unfinished email kept as a Gmail draft, never in our database: the editor saves it on its own a second after each change (no Save button; Discard deletes it), the agent's `create_draft`, or Gmail itself. Listed under Mail → Drafts, reopened in the editor; sending it from Fewmail or discarding it deletes it. Not the agent's reply drafts on Home. Free. |
| **Attachment** | A file of an email. The reader lists an email's files under it (an image shows its thumbnail) and opens each in a new tab, or downloads it when a browser cannot show it; images the email carries inside its body are drawn there, never listed twice. In spam an email loads nothing from the web (no image, pixel or font; a CSP in its frame) until **Show images**, a hover never warms its images, its files show no thumbnail and open only after a confirmation. The composer attaches files from the paperclip (`⇧⌘U`), a drop or a paste, up to 18 MB together; they are saved with the draft, survive Undo, and Forward carries the original's files. Free. |
| **Calendar** | `/calendar` (`G L`): the primary Google calendar of every mailbox, read live and never stored, as a day, week, month or year view. Events are added (`N`, or a press on empty time), edited and deleted there; saving a change to a recurring event asks what it reaches: this event, this and following events, or all events; the agent does the same through `list_events` / `create_event` / `update_event` / `delete_event`, each change waiting for the user's yes. Free. |
| **Mail views** | Layouts of the same inbox in Mail's view picker (`?filter=`, remembered per browser), never slices of it: **Focused** (the default) groups it by what the user does with it (Important, People, Calendar and travel, Money, Notifications, Newsletters, Promotions, Earlier); **Unread** groups unread mail that way and puts the rest under Read; **Categories** groups it by the agent's analysis category, mail that needs the user first (Security with every risk-flagged email, Person, Work, Finance, Receipt, Calendar, Travel, Notification, Newsletter, Promotion, then Other with mail never analysed), newest first inside each; **Inbox** is one list, newest first. Every grouped view folds a group to its newest five until Show all (never the last one, which pages on), offers Mark N read, puts a burst stack in its most important email's group and opens on the pinned threads. Web only: the agent's `list_mail` returns each email's category and filters by it, so it needs no layout of its own. Free. |
| **Folders** | A branch under Mail in the sidebar, open while Mail is (and the Folders group in ⌘K): Snoozed, Drafts, Archive, Spam and Trash open those Mail views (the Mail view picker holds only the inbox views), then the user's own Gmail labels (`mail.labels`, `listUserLabels` in `services/labels.ts`: read live from every mailbox, merged by name, never stored), each opening the Mail search for it (`label:name` in Gmail's own form). Free. |
| **Pin** | Keep a thread in sight: a pin is Gmail's star (`STARRED` on the thread, so starring in Gmail pins too and the other way round). Every inbox view opens on the inbox's pinned threads, one line each and only the first three until "Show all", and the rest of the list leaves them out (`listMail` filter `pinned`, `isPinned` on each row; the agent's `list_mail` takes the same filter). `S` pins or unpins the cursor row or the open thread. Mail rows pick for a batch (`X`, `⌘A`, a checkbox over the unread dot): archive, mark read (`⇧I`, also the cursor row alone), snooze, pin, trash or report spam all of them at once, one toast whose Undo takes them all back. Archiving takes a thread off the pinned list but keeps the star. Free. |
| **Snooze** | Hide a thread until a time; it comes back to the inbox unread. The menu offers Later today, Tomorrow, This weekend and Next week, each only while it is true (`SNOOZE_PRESETS`: no Later today past 21:00, no This weekend on a weekend), and Pick date & time (one `SnoozePicker` for every snooze menu). Free. |
| **Follow-up** | "Remind me if no reply in N days" on a sent email (Free: the thread resurfaces). The agent also sets follow-ups itself on sent mail that expects an answer (all plans); Pro adds a nudge on Home. |
| **Knowledge** | Free-form facts the user gives the agent (prices, availability…), used in drafts and autocomplete. Pro. |
| **Spam shield** | Community spam protection: "Report spam" (`!`) moves the thread to Gmail spam and votes; enough independent votes flag that sender's mail for everyone. Only a sender the user reported themselves goes to spam on its own (that report was their yes); everything else the shield catches (community votes, spam lists, patterns, two or more weak signals) stays in the inbox marked "Looks like spam" with a pending `spam` card on Home, and its yes moves it and votes (`proposeSpam` / `approveAction`). Votes also count against the campaign's patterns — the same text, the sending network, the linked domains — so a spammer gains nothing by switching address. "Not spam" votes the other way. Free. |
| **Maybe spam** | One weak signal (odd domain name, freshly registered domain, weak sender authentication): labelled "Looks like spam" and kept out of Important, never moved. Two or more → a `spam` card waiting for a yes (see **Spam shield**). Free. |
| **Snippets** | Saved texts inserted with `;name` in the editor. Free. |
| **Agent labels** | Optional `Fewmail/<Category>` Gmail labels so the sorting shows in any Gmail client. Free. |
| **Subscriptions** | `/subscriptions` (`G U`): every mailing list that wrote in the last 30 days and the user has not left, busiest first, with how many emails came and how many were opened (`isOpened`: Fewmail's own archive, which takes UNREAD off with the inbox and is remembered as `readByArchive`, and a rule's archive or mark-read are not opens; the same rule counts opens for Clear-out, new senders and Tidy's unsubscribe evidence). A row's name (`⏎` / `O`) opens those emails in Mail (`listSearch`, `in:anywhere`, since the count includes trashed and spam mail that Gmail's search leaves out). Unsubscribe (`U`) asks first (leaving a list has no undo), keeps the row spinning until the answer and leaves the list the way a person would: RFC 8058 one-click, else its unsubscribe page (opened from our server, a one-click form on it sent, success only when the page says so: `lib/unsubscribe-page.ts`), else its `mailto:` (a request sent from the mailbox the list wrote to; part of the unsubscribe, so it needs no other approval); a page that needs a person comes back as `link` and the user finishes there (a toast in the web, the link in the agent's answer) and archives everything that list sent; only an unsubscribe that went out makes later mail a zombie. A sender the network knows ignores unsubscribes (and without a List-Id) comes back as `ignoresUnsubscribe`: the web asks whether to block it (`offerBlock`, a trash rule for the sender), the agent offers `create_rule`; nothing blocks it unasked. A list is its titled List-Id (each GitHub repository is its own row, named by the List-Id's title when the sender sends several), else its sender. Free. |
| **Clear-out** | Moving the whole inbox backlog out at once (`scanClearOut` / `runClearOut` / `undoClearOut` in `services/cleanup.ts`). Groups (`CLEAR_OUT_GROUPS`): ads and social older than 3 days, read notifications older than 30 days, mailing lists with 3+ emails none opened (found in the newest 600 inbox emails' headers; also unsubscribed: the run leaves exactly the lists the scan showed, passed back as `lists`, never ones it finds itself), everything older than a year (off by default). Read live from Gmail, headers only, nothing stored; up to 50,000 emails per mailbox. Each run is archived under its own label, `Fewmail/Cleared/<UTC stamp>` (`run`), so Undo brings back that run only and drops its label (unsubscribes stay); the agent's `undo_clear_out` without a run takes the latest. A mailbox Gmail refused does not stop the others: the result says `failed` and the toast still offers Undo for what moved. The dialog opens from ⌘K only (never on its own: right after onboarding it fought the first sync for Gmail's quota); the agent has `preview_clear_out`, `clear_out` and `undo_clear_out`. Free. |
| **Wrapped** | `/wrapped` (⌘K, and the Time saved line on Home links there): the year or month so far (`Y` / `M`) in counts: mail in (from people, from lists and the rest, so the parts add up; people is by sender: all a sender wrote who wrote at least one personal email, except mailing-list mail, so their invoice counts too), handled on its own, replies sent from drafts, time saved, lists left, spam stopped, the busiest weekday and, separately, the busiest hour (shown as a range, 23:00–00:00), plus the three people who wrote most, counted the same way (in the app only). `getWrapped` in `services/wrapped.ts`; the agent's `get_wrapped`. Shared as a link `/w/<token>` whose token carries the counts signed with HMAC (no table, nothing stored, never a name or address) and as a 1080×1350 PNG drawn in the browser (`I`). Link previews: Caddy sends social crawlers on `/w/*` to the API's `/og/w/<token>` (Open Graph tags) whose image `/og/w/<token>/image.png` is drawn on the server (`services/wrapped-image.ts`, resvg-wasm, Inter and Lora from Google Fonts; the token carries the sharer's language, scripts those fonts lack fall back to English). Free. |
| **Reading** | Part of Home, never a page of its own, and kept as **daily issues**: one calendar day of newsletters (category `newsletter`, wherever they are; the starter rule archives them) in the user's time zone, written once the morning after and stored for good (`reading_issues`, one row per user and day: the newsletters it covers and the agent's summary, encrypted). Home shows the **fresh issue** (written in the last 24 h, so yesterday's from the morning hour until the next) as a card under the header; the day still filling is never shown, on Home or in the list. The card opens out of itself into a pane over Home's column (`/home?page=reading`; `N` on Home, `G N` and ⌘K anywhere: the latest issue; `/reading` redirects there for the links in digests already sent), no sidebar row. Under the issue, **Every issue** lists the others newest first; a row, a digest's or a notification's link open that day (`?day=YYYY-MM-DD`) over it or alone, and `esc` closes the day first, then the pane. With the agent an issue is **one summary of all its newsletters** (`writeReadingIssue`): an intro, up to four topics whose sentences end on the newsletters they came from (a badge that opens that newsletter), where several cover one thing said once with where they disagree, then up to two worth reading in full with why; promotions and newsletters with nothing new are left out and counted. It is written by one fast-model call from each newsletter's own summary (never the bodies), keeping their facts and numbers as written: it never adds a detail, never says one is missing and never pads a topic with a sentence that states no fact. The model's own source numbers ("[2]", "(n2)") and a topic title repeated at the start of its sentence are stripped when the summary is read (`withoutEchoes`). ▶ in an issue's head reads its summary aloud (`/voice/reading?day=`), folded or open. The issue's newsletters are listed under it (each marked in the summary, worth reading or left out); J / K walk the list and ⏎ opens one on its own page like any email, `esc` comes back to Reading, then closes it. While any of them is unread, **Mark all read** (`⇧I`, `reading.markRead` / `markReadingRead`, per day) ends the issue: the user's yes marks every newsletter in it read, thread by thread, and the toast's Undo marks them unread again (`mail.markUnread`); the Telegram and WhatsApp digest carries the same button (`readall:<day>`). On Free an issue is the list alone, each newsletter under its snippet, with no summary to hear. Each newsletter's own 2–3 sentence summary (`NEWSLETTER_SUMMARY`: every item it carries with its facts and numbers, nothing added) is written once, in one fast-model call, into the email's `analyses.summary`; on Free it is the snippet. The agent's `get_reading` (a day, else the latest issue), and the `reading` skill (`/reading` in Telegram and WhatsApp, an MCP prompt) turns it into a digest. **Newsletter digest** (`readingDigest`, on by default, Settings → Agent, with the morning hour, on every plan): at the morning hour (`briefHour`, 08:00 by default, in the user's time zone) the worker writes yesterday's issue for every user and sends it to Telegram and WhatsApp as one message (the day and count, topic titles in bold, the issue's link) (`readingReply`; outside WhatsApp's 24 h window the template's button `reading` fetches the latest issue), or, with neither connected (they are Pro), as a device notification (`readingNotification`) that opens the issue; nothing when the day had none. **A new user's first week**: right after the sign-in mailbox's first sync, Reading reads the week before sign-up: up to 100 emails of the last 8 days that mention unsubscribe, in any folder, are stored and judged by Jev alone (a newsletter keeps its analysis, with no card, rule, label or Tidy suggestion; nothing changes in Gmail), and an issue is written for each of the 7 days before today that had newsletters, the newest of them Home's fresh issue; no notification, and nothing when there were none. |
| **Voice** | ElevenLabs (`lib/elevenlabs.ts`, `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY`, optional `ELEVENLABS_VOICE_ID`), only on demand: ▶ on Reading speaks the newsletters' summary (`spokenReading`, MP3 from `/voice/reading`) — the only thing read aloud on the web; Home's queue is not, since the user wants the news, not their whole day; a voice message to the bot is answered with a voice note as well as text (Ogg Opus). A failed voice never drops the text. Pro. |
| **Zombie sender** | Mail from a list that keeps coming more than 14 days after you unsubscribed from that list; proposed for spam as a `spam` card, never moved without a yes. Free. |
| **Code** | A sign-in or verification code found in an incoming email (`oneTimeCode`, `apps/api/lib/one-time-code.ts`: a code word in the subject or the body's first 3,000 characters, links removed, then 4 to 8 digits or a short letters-and-digits code beside it; promo, zip and order numbers are not codes, nor a standard's number after its acronym (FSSC 22000, ISO 9001), and a code word inside another word ("hotpot", "footprint", "a one-time deal") does not count), kept encrypted in `messages.code` when the email is stored, before any analysis. The Mail row shows it to copy without opening the email (`Y` on the cursor row; in a burst stack, on that email's own line), with **Copy and archive** and **Copy and delete** in the chevron menu beside it the way iOS offers to delete a used code. Home opens with the codes of the last 15 minutes still in the inbox (`freshCodes`, at most 3, newest first) with the same split button, `Y` copying the newest, so a code never needs a trip to Mail; the agent's `list_mail` returns it and `get_today` lists the fresh ones (`codes`). Free. |
| **Bulk** | Mail sent near-identically to many people (`bulkCount`). Free. |
| **Risk** | Known phishing domain (public list), lookalike sender domain or changed payment details (from any sender, a Gmail address too); shown as a warning and a `security` action, the only card of that email, and its summary warns instead of repeating the request, whose yes ("I checked it") marks the email read and closes the card. Free. |
| **Commitment** | A promise you made in sent mail; due ones come back as actions. Pro. |
| **People** | Per-contact note the agent keeps, refreshed weekly, shown under a thread with the conversation count, which opens the person's latest 20 threads to jump to. Only for a person: someone the user wrote to or whose mail is `person` / `work` (`isPerson`); a service address gets no panel and no note. Pro. |
| **Board** | A page the user describes in their own words ("Trips: flights and hotels with what they cost"); the agent collects matching mail as **entries** (title, group, status, amount, date), starting with the history of its **period** — how far back it collects, picked when it is created and in its settings (`BOARD_PERIODS`: a day, a week, a month, 3 months (default), 6 months, a year; `boards.periodDays`). Entries that happened before the period leave every view of the board (entries, summary, groups, charts, banners, Home's digest) but stay stored, so a longer period brings them back; a longer period also reads the extra history, a shorter one reads nothing, and picks the **charts** when it is created — the user edits or removes them after. Onboarding offers ready-made **presets** (`BOARD_PRESETS`: Finances (payments, subscriptions with their renewals, bills; one board, since a separate Paid subscriptions put the same charge on two boards), Trips, Orders, Events, Invoices, Job search, Health, Home, Courses and school — compact three-column cards; the New board dialog offers the same as one-click fills — up to three, Finances and Trips picked by default, kept in `user_settings.starter_boards`; each preset brings its period, `BOARD_PRESET_PERIODS`: a year for Finances, Health and Home, 6 months for Courses, since yearly renewals and contracts are missed in 3 months); the worker creates them after the first sync as ordinary boards the user may change or delete. An email flagged as a risk (see **Risk**) never becomes an entry unless the user puts it on the board themselves. Each board may raise **banners**: `planBoard` picks `bannerDays` (renewals and bills 3, trips and events 2, null for none), the extractor records an entry's next date as `dueAt` (on a money board only a date money is due by or a renewal, never a delivery, and none for a paid one-off) and a status the board tracks (a money board tracks payment: a paid order stays paid when it ships), and `boardBanners` shows entries due within that window on Home (also in `get_today`) and on top of the board. The page opens on a **summary** (this month against last, what repeats per month, how many dates come up in the next 30 days, labelled Next 30 days and never as money due, since an Orders board counts its deliveries there), the heads-up banners, the charts, the **groups** (who or what: total, how often it repeats — weekly, monthly, quarterly, yearly, read from steady gaps and a steady price — and the next date, stated in an email or **expected** from that pace) and the entries; clicking a bar, a slice or a group filters the entries. The user corrects an entry by asking the agent (`edit_board_entry`; there is no edit form, the board is not a spreadsheet), takes it off the board (**Not on this board**, undoable; the board never collects that email again and the model learns from it), puts an email the search missed on a board (thread → More → Add to board), renames or merges groups, renames the board in place, changes what it collects (reads the history again; entries the user edited, added or took off stay) and the heads-up days — never the Gmail search itself: `planBoard` writes it from the description, so a hand-edited query cannot break a board, and orders or deletes boards (deleting confirms; it has no undo). Boards live in the sidebar (`G 1`…`G 9`, right-click for the same edits) and in ⌘K. Pro. |
| **Cleanup** | Weekly Sunday suggestion to clear accumulated noise. Pro. |
| **Agent requests** | No chat: the agent answers one request at a time (`askAgent` in `services/agent-run.ts`): a Telegram or WhatsApp text, a skill, or ⌘K's Ask when it is not a question about the mail. It answers from the mail through the same tools as MCP; anything that sends, changes Gmail, skips a card, adds a rule, changes the calendar or deletes a board never runs on its word: it becomes a **consent** card on Home (`proposeConsent`: kind `consent`, the tool, its input and what the server read for it, so the card shows every input; the reason says who asked, "The agent asks" or "Claude asks through MCP"), and the user's yes runs it (`approveCard` in `services/approve.ts`; a send gets the usual Undo). An MCP client's consent call does the same, with a push and a Telegram/WhatsApp link to Home. The messenger reply ends with "Decide on Home" when cards were left. Pro. |
| **Skill** | A built-in job the agent knows by name (`SKILLS`: `today`, `waiting`, `followups`, `bills`, `week`, `cleanup`, `reading`, `cancel`: the agent finds a paid service's cancel link or drafts a cancellation email it sends only after a yes; it never signs in or pays), run as `/name` plus optional details. A Telegram command, a WhatsApp text and an MCP prompt all start the same skill. Not user-defined. Pro. |
| **Instructions** | Free-text or voice messages to the Telegram or WhatsApp bot; replying to a bot message targets its thread. Pro. |

UI copy says "the agent" or "Fewmail", never "AI" or "the model". No em dashes (—) in copy, in any language, nor in prompts and tool descriptions the models read: a comma, colon, period or parentheses instead.

| **Free** | Forever. **One allowance, never degrade.** Every paid plan has every feature and the same models; they differ only in `PLAN_ALLOWANCE_USD`: what the agent may cost per month, sized so a yearly plan keeps 20% (Max 10%, so its users get more of the price) after Stripe, the allowance spent and a busy inbox through Jev. Everything the agent does is paid from it at its real price (`charge`: model calls at `MODEL_PRICES`, reading aloud, transcription, OCR, autocomplete), so a feature the user never opens costs them nothing; there are no separate email, message, board or task limits. The user sees it as a share (`usedPercent`) and in round numbers of emails or messages (`allowanceIn`, from `TYPICAL_COST_USD`), never in dollars. Noise Jev settles is free. Once it is spent new mail is handled exactly as on Free (Jev, rules, spam shield) with no summary or draft, except urgent mail, which may run 10% over (`URGENT_RESERVE`); mail that needs the user still gets its Home card, unwritten; anything the user asks of the agent (Ask, Telegram / WhatsApp texts and voice, reading aloud, a new board) is refused with the reset date (`requireBudget`), and background work (board entries and history, people notes, promises, the Reading summary, autocomplete, plain-language search) waits (`hasBudget`). It is monthly on yearly plans too (never a yearly pool) and resets on the 1st (UTC).

**Never push.** Paid plans are mentioned only in Settings → Plan (the usage meter and the plan choices), one ⌘K item ("Upgrade to Pro"), a muted "N days left" line in the sidebar during the trial, and a muted usage line once a month is ≥ 90% spent, the upgrade dialog when a Free user opens a Pro feature, and on Free Home's hints, which open with what Pro adds (Write for me, Ask your mail, Boards; one a day, each dismissed for good, like every hint). No modals that open on their own, no banners, no badges, no "Sent with Fewmail" signature.
