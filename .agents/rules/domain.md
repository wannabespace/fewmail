# Domain

Fewmail is an **email agent**: it reads every incoming email so the person does not have to, and shows them only what needs them. The UI is built around decisions, not around the inbox.

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Mailbox** | A Gmail account whose mail Fewmail reads. One is the account you sign in with; more can be added in Settings and their mail joins the same Home, queue and inbox; Mail can be narrowed to one of them. An added mailbox never signs anyone in to your account — signing in with it opens its own Fewmail. An added one can be **turned off** (its switch in Settings → Mailboxes) instead of removed: Fewmail does not read it and nothing of it is shown or acted on (mail, cards, promises, reminders, calendar, contacts, drafts, the From field) until it is turned on again, when everything comes back and what arrived meanwhile is read; only mail the user already scheduled from it still goes out. The sign-in mailbox is always on. |
| **Home** | `/home` (`G H`): the open **bot**'s chat (`?bot=<id>`), the bots themselves one row each in the sidebar's **Bots** section under the places, on every page; there is no Home row, Fewmail's chat is where Home opens (`G H`), live as mail arrives. The app itself opens on **Mail**, not Home: sign-in, the landing for a returning user, the logo, the installed app's and the Mac app's first window all go to `/mail`. Fewmail's latest message is **What happened**, computed in the browser (no model text) and said like any bot's message, its time at its end: "The agent is reading 3 new emails" while a sync is under way (never during the first read, whose headline "Reading your inbox…" and spinner line already say it; `reading` counts unanalysed inbox mail of the last day only while a mailbox's `syncStartedAt` is set), a headline counting what waits for the user in every bot's chat ("Seven things need you.", "All done. Nothing needs you." once none is left; every pending card but a tidy proposal, `waitsForUser` / `useWaiting` in the browser and `today.count` / `countWaiting` in SQL: the one count a chat's bar, a bot's profile, the sidebar and the tab's title all say), a chip per bot that holds some under it (its face, name and count; a click opens its chat), since Fewmail speaks for every bot, one sentence ("48 emails in the last day. Mostly newsletters and notifications, and just 5 from people. Your bots took care of 6.", the middle part only when fewer than half came from people) and the calendar's events left today and tomorrow as chips. **Digest**: each bot's report of a day, kept in its chat as history (`writeDigests`, `services/home.ts`; a `digest` post, no model): right after the first read of the account's own mailbox and then every morning at `MORNING_HOUR` (08:00 in the user's time zone), only when it has news (`DIGEST_GAP_HOURS`): Fewmail's when a card of its own came since its last digest, or once mail came and its last digest is 12 hours old, with What happened's counts as they were then (`counts`), so the first read and the next morning are one report; another bot's only while cards of its own wait that its last digest did not have; it keeps the waiting cards as ids, so an older digest shows its frozen headline and sentence with each card's status as it is now. Fewmail's latest digest, while no report came after it, is What happened itself, live, never drawn twice; older ones stay in the chat as they were (two weeks a page, **Show earlier**), so the user scrolls back through the days. Then every bot, in order **Fewmail**, then the user's bots newest first (the **Board** last, being the oldest), each with its latest line, when, and how much is new since the user last looked at it (`bots.chats`; the filled count is muted for a muted bot), under **New bot** at the top. Without `?bot` Home opens the first bot with something new, else Fewmail; `[` / `]` step through them, `/` writes to the open one. Below `lg` the list is a row of the bots' faces over the chat. **A bot's chat** (`bots.chat`, `services/bot-chat.ts`): what it reported, read from the tables that hold it and never copied (its cards in bursts, each burst one message saying what waits, what it did on its own, what was done after a yes, skipped or refused by Gmail, with the settled cards' emails under it (a card that waits is only counted there: its row is in Waiting now), read as they are now, so a yes in Telegram settles the message too; the Board's promises as they were written down), what the user wrote and the answers, what it said it would come back with (**Nudge**) and its **Digest** of each day, oldest first, two weeks at a time (**Show earlier**). Under the chat, **Waiting now**: what the bot waits on, Home's working list for its mail: its pending cards grouped by kind as before (`J/K`, `⏎` opens a card's email in the sidebar with its proposal, `X`, `⌘A`, `⇧⏎`, `S`, `⌫`, Undo, stale cards' **Skip them** `⇧S`, every group but the last folded to five), its emails to tidy (**Tidy**, below), and its mail **Worth knowing**. Fewmail's also holds what is nobody's mail: sent mail awaiting an answer, **new senders** and **suggested bots**. A card made by a bot of the user's says so ("Your bot: …"). Opening a bot marks its chat read (`bots.read`). **Introduction**: once, right after the first read of the account's own mailbox (`introduceUser`, `services/bot-chat.ts`; with the agent and its budget only, so Free never gets it), Fewmail's chat opens with two to four sentences on who the user seems to be (work or role, company or field, who they write with, what fills the inbox) from their latest sent mail and mail from people (`person` / `work`), in their language, and asks them to correct it; it is an `answer` post, so the next reply reads it back with the correction. Internal names stay `today.*` (oRPC) and `get_today` (MCP clients depend on it). **Catch-up window** (`catchUp`, `services/home.ts`): Home's sentence covers everything since the user's last day on Home (`user_settings.todaySince`, moved on the first look on a new local day), at least 24 h and at most 7 days ("in the last 3 days"). **Tidy**: quiet inbox mail (importance < 2) Jev would archive, mark read or unsubscribe from (`analyses.suggestedAction`, only when those three reach 0.7 of its odds, `NOISE_CONFIDENCE`), that no bot takes or a tidying bot asks about (`listTidy`): only a thread's newest email, never one with a card or already acted on; an unsubscribe is offered only for a sender the user plainly ignores, else as an archive. It waits in its bot's chat with **Approve all** (`T`) and one by one (`⏎` approves, `S` keeps it, a `dismissed` action so it never comes back); nothing moves until the user approves. **Worth knowing**: the other unread inbox mail of importance ≥ 1 from the catch-up window, dated mail no card shows (a thread's newest dated email; an ISO due date sorts soonest first and leaves once passed) and, in Fewmail's, sent mail still awaiting an answer; a conversation sits in one block only, never an email with a code. **Suggested bot** (`botSuggestions` / `declineBotSuggestion`): a sender whose every analysed email of the last 30 days, at least three, the agent would archive or mark read, none important, none handled by a bot or kept by the user, at most three; **Always archive** / **Always mark read** makes a bot with a simple `from` match, **No, thanks** keeps the sender's keyed hash (`declined_bot_suggestions`). **New sender** (`newSenders` / `keepSender`): a mailing list whose first email is at most 14 days old, that wrote at least three times and was opened at most once; **Always archive** (`A`), **Unsubscribe** (`U`) or **Keep as usual** (`I`); the card opens the sender's emails beside the chat (`V`). **Once asked, it stays in the chat until answered** (for 14 days): opening those emails fails "opened at most once", so the asked senders are remembered with the counts they were asked with (`user_settings.askedSenders`, by list hash and email id, never addresses) and come back whatever was read since. There are no Home cards, panes or Rules page any more: each was a bot's job without its chat. |
| **Queue** | Pending **actions** (`actions.status = 'pending'`) whose email is out of Trash and Spam (`notBinned`: the queue, Home's count and the notice bar alike; a binned email's card stays pending and comes back with it on Undo), ordered by importance, then mail someone waits for an answer to, then age. Each is approved (`⏎`), edited, redrafted or skipped (`S`, or by archiving its email anywhere — web, MCP, the agent: `archiveMessage` skips the thread's pending cards but promises, follow-ups and consents (`OUTLIVE_ARCHIVE_KINDS`), and the archive's Undo restores them); `⇧⏎` approves every pending card of the selected one's kind at once (`approveCards`: one by one, each send with its own undo window). Batch approval is the answer to repetitive replies: nothing is ever sent without a yes. Telegram and WhatsApp end the queue with one button per kind that has two or more cards (`all:<kind>`). |
| **Action** | Something the agent proposes or did: `reply` (with a draft), `pay`, `schedule`, `follow_up`, `read`, `archive`, `mark_read`, `unsubscribe`, `spam`, `security`, `commitment`, `heads_up` (see **Heads-up**), `cleanup`, `consent` (an agent tool waiting for a yes, see **Agent requests**). A card's button never promises what Fewmail does not do. A `pay` or `read` card can carry the email's own link (`payload.link`: pay, sign, renew, confirm or read, shown with its host): ⏎ opens it in a new tab and the yes moves to `M`, saying what the user did ("I paid it", `actionDone`). A `schedule` card whose meeting has an exact start (`payload.event`) is approved as Add to calendar, which creates the event in the email's mailbox calendar; a `reply` card carries the same event for its own Add to calendar button and Send never adds it. ⏎ on a `follow_up` card writes the reminder, turning it into a `reply` card that still needs its own yes to send. Elsewhere the yes on those kinds only marks the email read and closes the card, and says so ("I scheduled it"). Statuses: `pending`, `done`, `dismissed` (skipped), `auto_done` (by a bot acting on its own), `failed`. An email with a **code** never gets a card; the thread view shows each settled card (done, auto_done, failed) beside its own email, never as a list above the thread; pending ones live on Home. |
| **Handled** | What the agent did: `done` (after the user's yes), `auto_done` (by a bot the user let act on its own, or a sender they reported) and `failed`. Always reviewable, never hidden: each bot's chat shows what it did on its own and after a yes, and the agent reads all of it with `list_handled`. The **journal** ("Done in your mail", `components/journal.tsx`, unfolded from one quiet line at the end of Fewmail Bot's chat: "35 changes in your mail in the last 7 days · Show") lists the last 7 days by day, five per day until **Show all N**, with the email, what was done (a consent's yes is recorded as what its tool did, `recordDone`: a Gmail move becomes that kind, so it reads like a bot's and gets its undo), why, who allowed it ("You said yes" or "Your bot: …"), its time and an undo where one exists (archive, spam; it shows on hover over the time and leaves once used), failures first. Without a yes or a bot acting on its own the agent only reads. |
| **Time saved** | `timeSaved` (`services/mail.ts`): handled mail × 15 s plus replies sent from the agent's drafts × 3 min (`SECONDS_SAVED`), over the last 7 days. A muted line on Home whose tooltip gives both counts, `saved` in the agent's `get_today`, and on Mondays the **weekly report**: its own message in Telegram and WhatsApp at the morning hour (`weekReply`; outside WhatsApp's 24 h window the template's button `week` fetches it), nothing when no time was saved. Never inflated: only those two counts. |
| **Analysis** | Per-message result (Jev decides; on Pro the fast model writes the text, on Free the summary is the snippet): category, importance 0–3, one-line summary, reason, `needsReply`, extracted amount (a plain number and its ISO code, `1250.00 USD`, so the app formats it in the UI language; older free-text amounts show as written) / due date / meeting time, matched bot. When the LLM writes the text of mail that needs the user (importance ≥ 2), a bill or a ticket (`finance`, `travel`; `worthReading`), the email's PDFs and images (≤ 2, ≤ 10 MB, first 3 pages, `services/attachments.ts`, Mistral OCR) are read into its prompt and never stored. |
| **Importance** | 3 urgent: security, money overdue or due within two days, a service about to be suspended (a Google Cloud bill past due), a person blocked on the user (pushed to Telegram and WhatsApp immediately, each switchable in Settings → Notifications), 2 needs you soon, 1 worth knowing, 0 noise. |
| **Bot** | A chat on Home that looks after some of the user's mail. Each chat in the sidebar has the same menu on its ⋯ and its right click: **Pin** (`pinnedBots` in `user_settings`: kept at the top under Fewmail Bot, a pin glyph beside the name), **Move to folder** (the user's own folders, `botFolders`, encrypted, at most `BOT_FOLDERS_MAX`, each bot in one at most; a folder is a foldable row with Rename and Delete, deleting it keeps its bots), **Rename** and **Delete** (every bot but Fewmail Bot, the Board too), and **Clear history** (`clearBotChat`: its posts go for good and `botsClearedAt` keeps its older cards out of the chat, while what it did in the mail and its nudges still to come stay). Web only, the sidebar's own order; the agent reads none of it. Fewmail Bot's menu has neither Pin nor Move to folder: it is pinned for good (`listBotChats` says so) and in no folder, and `requireMovable` refuses both. The first is always **Fewmail Bot** (the system bot `fewmail`, named so in every language): the main bot, like Telegram's BotFather, which has no settings (only mute), makes the user's other bots when asked (`create_bot`), finds emails, answers questions about the mail and keeps whatever none of their bots takes; Home opens on its chat. A bot of the user's has a name (always: one made from a sender takes the sender's name), which mail is its (a plain-language `instruction` Jev matches, or a simple `match` on sender, subject or any mailing list, matched without a model), what it does with that mail (`effect`) and whether it acts on its own (`enabled`; off, it asks for a yes on each email). Its settings take which mail and what to do with it in one text ("Mark invoices from Hetzner read"; a sender's bot keeps its match, and its text only says what to do): the text is the only place the effect shows, so there is no field or line for it: saving reads it from the words as they stand (`readBotEffect`, at least 0.6 odds, 0.8 for trash and unsubscribe; Jev already reads them once typing pauses, so saving rarely waits), words left as they were keep the bot's own effect, and changed words that do not say what to do only collect. `create_bot` writes the effect into the instruction for the same reason ("Invoices from Hetzner: mark them read"). Stored in `bots`. Jev picks each incoming email's bot from the user's bots in one decision (`analyses.botId`); a card the bot made carries it too (`actions.botId`); mail no bot takes is **Fewmail**'s. Effects: `archive`, `mark_read`, `unsubscribe`, `trash` move each email (on their own only below importance 2; mail that needs the user waits as a card for a yes), `tidy` does what Jev suggests for each email (archive, mark read or unsubscribe; asking, its mail waits in its Tidy), `draft_reply` leaves a draft, `always_show` keeps the email on Home (importance at least 2), `collect` only gathers it in the chat. A simple bot's match needs no model and wins over Jev's pick (`simpleBotOf` in `services/agent.ts`), but Jev still judges the email like any bot's, so a simple bot also moves only mail below importance 2 on its own and, asking, puts each email up for a yes; when Jev fails it acts as it always did. Only Fewmail Bot's chat has `create_bot`: another bot sends the user there for a new one. Only one bot is not the user's, and it can never be deleted: **Fewmail** (`fewmail`, all mail no bot takes, the agent itself); there is no list of system bots, a check is `bot === 'fewmail'`. Every other bot is a `bots` row made from words or from a **preset** (`BOT_PRESETS`, named in the user's language from `starterBots`): the first sign-in seeds all four, and New bot offers them as **Templates** above its fields (`bots.addPreset`; one click makes the bot and opens its chat): the **Board** (effect `promises`, at most one, offered only while the user has none), **Important** (`always_show`), **Can wait** (`collect`) and **Quick** (`tidy`, asking until the user lets it act); the words of Important and Quick end with what they do, so rewriting them keeps it. Made, changed and deleted from Home (**New bot**, the chat's profile, or by asking in a chat: the bot changes itself with `update_bot`, which runs at once, so "ask me first", "also take Notion" or "pause for a week" is saved without a card; only letting it act on its own (`enabled: true`) is a consent like `create_bot`, since that moves mail (`toolAccess`)); deleting one deletes its chat, and its mail goes to Fewmail. A bot's chat can be muted (no notifications, `user_settings.mutedBots`). A bot of the user's can be **paused** until a moment (`bots.pausedUntil`: tomorrow 8:00, a week, a month, or any moment through `update_bot`): until then Jev and the simple bots skip it (`isWorking`) and its mail is Fewmail's; it works again on its own once the moment passes, or on **Resume**. **Above the composer**, while it is empty, up to three things to send the bot in a tap, written by the agent for its chat as it stands (`suggestQuestions` in `services/agent-run.ts`, fast model, charged to the allowance like any call; kept in memory until someone says something in the chat, the bot or the language changes, so arriving mail and reopening a chat cost nothing; none on Free, past the allowance or while the bot answers). **Every bot says what it is and what it is doing**: its chat begins with its intro (computed, no model: Fewmail's and the Board's paragraph, a bot of the user's "Hi, I'm …", its words and whether it acts on its own or asks; every intro says the bot can remind the user of something (a **Nudge**, the Board on a due day) and can handle its mail without them once let act on its own), its bar says what it is doing now (Fewmail reading N new emails, paused until, N things waiting for the user, else how it works and when it last wrote), what it looks after is in its intro and its profile (no strip under the bar), and **What I do** (`P`) opens its profile beside the chat: the last 7 days (on its own, with the user's approval, waiting), the last things it did, its words, Acts on its own, the pause, notifications and Delete. In the sidebar each bot is a messenger's chat row, its latest message under its name. As in a messenger, a chat opens at the first thing its bot said since the user last looked (`unreadSince` from `listBotChats`, kept from the moment it opened since opening reads it), at its intro when all of it is new, and at its end when nothing is new or what is new fits. On Free every effect but `draft_reply` and `always_show`. |
| **Language** | One setting for the whole product, saved on the first sign-in from the browser's language, or the one picked on the login page (there is no onboarding step; `_app.tsx`'s `beforeLoad` makes the first `settings.save`, which seeds the presets in it and starts the first read), changed later in Settings: the app UI, Telegram bot, summaries, reasons, action labels and the presets' names use it. **Replies always use the language of the email being answered.** The public site follows the visitor's browser language, switchable in its footer, and the choice carries into the login page and the account. Legal documents stay in English (the binding version) with a translated note. |
| **Voice** | How drafts sound: learned from the user's recent sent mail (synced alongside the inbox) plus an optional `voice` note. |
| **Saved draft** | An unfinished email kept as a Gmail draft, never in our database: the editor saves it on its own a second after each change (no Save button; Discard deletes it), and one erased back to nothing (a reply: back to its filled-in To and Re: subject) is deleted rather than kept empty, the agent's `create_draft`, or Gmail itself. Listed under Mail → Drafts, reopened in the editor; sending it from Fewmail or discarding it deletes it. Not the agent's reply drafts on Home. Free. |
| **Attachment** | A file of an email. The reader lists an email's files under it (an image shows its thumbnail) and opens each in a new tab, or downloads it when a browser cannot show it; images the email carries inside its body are drawn there, never listed twice. In spam an email loads nothing from the web (no image, pixel or font; a CSP in its frame) until **Show images**, a hover never warms its images, its files show no thumbnail and open only after a confirmation. The composer attaches files from the paperclip (`⇧⌘U`), a drop or a paste, up to 18 MB together; they are saved with the draft, survive Undo, and Forward carries the original's files. Free. |
| **Calendar** | `/calendar` (`G L`): the primary Google calendar of every mailbox, read live and never stored, as a day, week, month or year view. Events are added (`N`, or a press on empty time), edited and deleted there; saving or deleting a recurring event asks what it reaches: this event, this and following events, or all events; the agent does the same through `list_events` / `create_event` / `update_event` / `delete_event`, each change waiting for the user's yes. Free. |
| **Mail views** | Layouts of the same inbox in Mail's view picker (`?filter=`, remembered per browser), never slices of it: **Focused** (the default) groups it by what the user does with it (Important, People, Calendar and travel, Money, Notifications, Newsletters, Promotions, Earlier); **Unread** groups unread mail that way and puts the rest under Read; **Categories** groups it by the agent's analysis category, mail that needs the user first (Security with every risk-flagged email, Person, Work, Finance, Receipt, Calendar, Travel, Notification, Newsletter, Promotion, then Other with mail never analysed), newest first inside each; **Inbox** is one list, newest first. Every grouped view folds a group to its newest five until Show all (never the last one, which pages on), offers Mark N read and opens on the pinned threads. Web only: the agent's `list_mail` returns each email's category and filters by it, so it needs no layout of its own. Free. |
| **Folders** | A branch under Mail in the sidebar, open while Mail is or a saved draft is being edited: Snoozed, Sent, Drafts, Archive, Spam and Trash open those folders, each its own page (`/mail/sent`, `/mail/spam`…, `components/mail-page.tsx` behind thin routes; an old `/mail?filter=spam` redirects there) under its name, while the Mail view picker and `[ / ]` hold only the inbox views; **Sent** lists what the user sent, newest first, a row nobody answered named by whom it went to ("To anna@…"; recipients come with `list_mail` for `sent` only), and Drafts carries its count (`mail.draftCount`: Gmail's own total plus failed sends, never every draft read), then the user's own Gmail labels (`mail.labels`, `listUserLabels` in `services/labels.ts`: read live from every mailbox, merged by name, never stored), each opening the Mail search for it (`label:name` in Gmail's own form). Free. |
| **Pin** | Keep a thread in sight: a pin is Gmail's star (`STARRED` on the thread, so starring in Gmail pins too and the other way round). Every inbox view opens on the inbox's pinned threads, one line each and only the first three until "Show all", and the rest of the list leaves them out (`listMail` filter `pinned`, `isPinned` on each row; the agent's `list_mail` takes the same filter). `S` pins or unpins the cursor row or the open thread. Mail rows pick for a batch (`X`, `⌘A`, a checkbox over the unread dot): archive, mark read (`⇧I`, also the cursor row alone), snooze, pin, trash or report spam all of them at once, one toast whose Undo takes them all back. Archiving takes a thread off the pinned list but keeps the star. Free. |
| **Snooze** | Hide a thread until a time; it comes back to the inbox unread. Snoozed also lists what the user snoozed in Gmail itself (no wake time is known for those; Gmail brings them back, and Unsnooze ends Gmail's snooze too). The menu offers Later today, Tomorrow, This weekend and Next week, each only while it is true (`SNOOZE_PRESETS`: no Later today past 21:00, no This weekend on a weekend), and Pick date & time (one `SnoozePicker` for every snooze menu). Free. |
| **Follow-up** | "Remind me if no reply in N days" on a sent email (Free: the thread resurfaces). The agent also sets follow-ups itself on sent mail that expects an answer (all plans); Pro adds a nudge on Home. |
| **Knowledge** | Free-form facts the user gives the agent (prices, availability…), used in drafts and autocomplete. Pro. |
| **Spam shield** | Community spam protection: "Report spam" (`!`) moves the thread to Gmail spam and votes; enough independent votes flag that sender's mail for everyone. Only a sender the user reported themselves goes to spam on its own (that report was their yes); everything else the shield catches (community votes, spam lists, patterns, two or more weak signals) stays in the inbox marked "Looks like spam" with a pending `spam` card on Home, and its yes moves it and votes (`proposeSpam` / `approveAction`). Votes also count against the campaign's patterns — the same text, the sending network, the linked domains — so a spammer gains nothing by switching address. "Not spam" votes the other way. Free. |
| **Maybe spam** | One weak signal (odd domain name, freshly registered domain, weak sender authentication): labelled "Looks like spam" and kept out of Important, never moved. Two or more → a `spam` card waiting for a yes (see **Spam shield**). Free. |
| **Snippets** | Saved texts inserted with `;name` in the editor. Free. |
| **Agent labels** | Optional `Fewmail/<Category>` Gmail labels so the sorting shows in any Gmail client. Free. |
| **Mailing lists** | `/subscriptions` (`G U`; internal names stay `subscriptions`), uk «Розсилки», never "Subscriptions": people read it as paid subscriptions, and its lead never mentions spam, which read as the page moving mail to spam. Every mailing list that wrote in the last 30 days and the user has not left, busiest first, with how many emails came and how many were opened (`isOpened`: Fewmail's own archive, which takes UNREAD off with the inbox and is remembered as `readByArchive`, and a bot's archive or mark-read are not opens; the same rule counts opens for Clear-out, new senders and Tidy's unsubscribe evidence). A row's name (`⏎` / `O`) opens those emails in Mail (`listSearch`, `in:anywhere`, since the count includes trashed and spam mail that Gmail's search leaves out). Unsubscribe (`U`) asks first (leaving a list has no undo), keeps the row spinning until the answer and leaves the list the way a person would: RFC 8058 one-click, else its unsubscribe page (opened from our server, a one-click form on it sent, success only when the page says so: `lib/unsubscribe-page.ts`), else its `mailto:` (a request sent from the mailbox the list wrote to; part of the unsubscribe, so it needs no other approval); a page that needs a person comes back as `link` and the user finishes there (a toast in the web, the link in the agent's answer) and archives everything that list sent; only an unsubscribe that went out makes later mail a zombie. A sender the network knows ignores unsubscribes (and without a List-Id) comes back as `ignoresUnsubscribe`: the web asks whether to block it (`offerBlock`, a trash bot for the sender), the agent offers `create_bot`; nothing blocks it unasked. A list is its titled List-Id (each GitHub repository is its own row, named by the List-Id's title when the sender sends several), else its sender. A list enough Fewmail users get (10, `MIN_READERS`) shows the share of its readers who left it ("62% of its readers on Fewmail left it", `leftShare`) on its row and in Home's new senders: `list_sightings` keeps keyed hashes of the list and the user, never more. Free. |
| **Clear-out** | Moving the whole inbox backlog out at once (`scanClearOut` / `runClearOut` / `undoClearOut` in `services/cleanup.ts`). Groups (`CLEAR_OUT_GROUPS`): ads and social older than 3 days, read notifications older than 30 days, mailing lists with 3+ emails none opened (found in the newest 600 inbox emails' headers; also unsubscribed: the run leaves exactly the lists the scan showed, passed back as `lists`, never ones it finds itself), everything older than a year (off by default). Read live from Gmail, headers only, nothing stored; up to 50,000 emails per mailbox. Each run is archived under its own label, `Fewmail/Cleared/<UTC stamp>` (`run`), so Undo brings back that run only and drops its label (unsubscribes stay); the agent's `undo_clear_out` without a run takes the latest. A mailbox Gmail refused does not stop the others: the result says `failed` and the toast still offers Undo for what moved. There is no button for it any more (a bot tidies as mail comes): the agent runs it when asked, in Fewmail's chat, Telegram or MCP, with `preview_clear_out`, `clear_out` and `undo_clear_out`. Free. |
